## 1. Stage purpose

This stage examines the five books selected in Stage 2 as sources in their own right, extracts independently expressed methods and limitations, reconciles their overlap and tensions, and derives a provisional legal-production capability model.

The objective is not to summarize five books. It is to answer:

> What reusable production behaviour changes when the methods in these sources are applied to evidence-backed legal work?

The Stage 2 corpus is:

1. Emily Finch and Stefan Fafinski, *Legal Skills*, 10th ed. (Oxford University Press, 2025)
2. Emily Allbon, Ian Hunter and Peter Clinch, *Legal Research: A Practitioner’s Handbook*, 3rd ed. (Wildy, Simmonds & Hill, 2019)
3. Frederick Schauer, *Thinking Like a Lawyer: A New Introduction to Legal Reasoning* (Harvard University Press, 2009)
4. Kenneth A. Adams, *A Manual of Style for Contract Drafting*, 4th ed. (American Bar Association, 2017)
5. Antoine Masson, Hugues Bouthinon-Dumas, Jean-Michel Do Carmo Silva and W. Gregory Voss (eds.), *The Oxford Handbook of Law and Management* (Oxford University Press, 2026)

The extraction is constrained by the Stage 1 boundary. It is interested in reusable legal-production method, not in promoting jurisdiction-specific substantive propositions into durable prompts.

## 2. Governing research contract

This stage follows the Production Skills domain-research process:

- examine every selected source sufficiently for its intended contribution;
- distinguish full-text access from relevant-excerpt access;
- do not treat publisher descriptions, summaries or model memory as direct-source examination;
- relevant excerpts may be sufficient when they cover the intended contribution with enough context to expose assumptions and limitations;
- record what was actually examined;
- express findings independently rather than reproducing proprietary text;
- preserve source locations;
- keep source ideas separate from eventual skill architecture;
- record overlap, tensions, limitations and unresolved claims;
- do not promote unresolved claims into unconditional core rules.

The two user-supplied summary-only files were therefore **not** treated as full-book access. They were expanded through direct web-accessible material from the books or their publishers.

No supplied book text, summary PDF or substantial copyrighted extract is committed to this repository.

# 4. Source 1 — Finch & Fafinski, *Legal Skills*

## 4.1 Material examined

### Direct full-text predecessor material

User-supplied *Legal Skills*, 3rd edition (Oxford University Press, 2011), with targeted examination of:

- Introduction;
- Part I — Sources of law;
- chapters on finding and using legislation;
- chapters on finding and using cases;
- Chapter 14 — Answering problem questions;
- Chapter 18 — Negotiation skills.

Material locations used include:

```text
Introduction: pp. 1–3
Detailed contents: pp. xix–xxi
Using legislation / statutory interpretation: around pp. 75–88
Using cases: around pp. 141–168
Answering problem questions: pp. 343–364
Negotiation skills: pp. 451–472
```

### Current-edition reconciliation material

The 10th edition was checked through current web-accessible source material:

- Oxford Learning Link — `https://learninglink.oup.com/access/finch10e`
- Google Books — `https://books.google.com/books/about/Legal_Skills.html?id=BpQI0QEACAAJ`
- Google Books text-view record — `https://books.google.com/books/about/Legal_Skills.html?id=tAReEQAAQBAJ`
- 10th-edition bibliographic / contents pages from Oxford-linked booksellers
- Chapter 15, “Answering problem questions”, DOI `10.1093/he/9780198924722.003.0015`
- Chapter 4, “Using legislation”, 10th-edition chapter metadata / abstract

The 10th edition retains the same broad production-relevant spine but now explicitly includes:

```text
legal reasoning and ethics
drafting skills
employability skills
updated Brexit / retained-EU-law coverage
```

The current chapter description for “Answering problem questions” continues to emphasize identifying issues, applying law to new factual situations and supporting analysis with authority.

## 4.2 Finding: legal work starts by structuring the problem, not searching

The problem-solving chapter uses a four-part structure:

```text
identify the question
→ state the relevant law
→ work out how the law operates on the facts
→ reach a conclusion
```

The same chapter stresses that facts should be decomposed into manageable issues and subissues. The useful production principle is not the academic acronym or assessment context; it is the sequencing constraint:

```text
unstructured matter
→ question / issue decomposition
→ relevant law
→ application to material facts
→ bounded conclusion
```

### Applicability

Retain for matter framing, issue spotting, research planning and analysis.

### Limitation

A production system needs additional dimensions absent from a simple law-school problem answer:

```text
jurisdiction
time / valid-as-of date
fact status vs assumption
authority status
uncertainty
risk / options
project action
review / escalation
```

The four-part method is therefore a useful kernel, not the complete Legal Skills workflow.

## 4.3 Finding: propositions should be supported by appropriate authority

The problem-question guidance moves from orientation material toward cases and legislation and expects propositions of law to be supported by authority.

The “using cases” material makes clear that working with a case requires more than citing its name. Relevant concerns include:

- court and hierarchy;
- material facts;
- ratio decidendi;
- obiter dicta;
- whether the precedent is binding or persuasive;
- whether the case has been reversed, overruled, distinguished or otherwise affected.

### Production implication

A legal proposition should not be represented as one opaque sentence plus one citation. It should carry enough evidence to answer:

```text
what proposition?
from what source?
what is the source's legal status?
which part of the source supports it?
what facts / conditions make it applicable?
is the source still current and authoritative for this matter?
```

## 4.4 Finding: source retrieval and source use are separate skills

The book separates:

```text
finding legislation
from
using legislation

finding cases
from
using cases
```

This is a useful architectural distinction.

Retrieval success is not legal-analysis success. A system can find the correct statute or case and still misunderstand its status, operative text, ratio, applicability or current effect.

### Production implication

Later skills should preserve separate evidence for:

```text
retrieval
verification
proposition extraction
application
```

without necessarily exposing them as four separate user-facing skills.

## 4.5 Finding: application is the value-producing step

The problem-solving material explicitly treats application of law to facts as the decisive part of a legal problem answer.

For Legal Skills this means:

```text
source found
≠
question answered
```

The system must show how material facts satisfy, fail or complicate the elements of the relevant rule or proposition.

## 4.6 Finding: interpretation needs both text discipline and context

The statutory-interpretation material starts from the authority of enacted words but examines literal, purposive and other interpretive approaches.

The durable production lesson is not to hard-code the 2011 account of UK statutory interpretation. It is:

- begin with the operative legal text;
- distinguish text from explanatory/background material;
- identify ambiguity or interpretive choice;
- determine which interpretive methods the relevant legal system permits;
- do not silently replace the enacted rule with the analyst's preferred policy outcome.

The 10th-edition description confirms that interpretation remains a first-class skill while updating the post-Brexit / assimilated-EU-law context.

## 4.7 Finding: practical legal work requires rehearsal, feedback and reflection

The book repeatedly uses practical exercises and post-task reflection rather than presenting skill as passive knowledge.

For Legal Skills this supports evaluation through realistic matters and repair loops, not only knowledge quizzes.

## 4.8 Finding: negotiation starts before the meeting

The negotiation chapter divides the activity into preparation and execution. Preparation includes:

```text
fact analysis
research
strategy formulation
```

before the live negotiation.

### Production implication

Drafting, negotiation or advice should not be started merely because a user requests a document. The system should first determine whether facts, authority and objectives are sufficiently established.

## 4.9 Disposition

**Retain / adapt.**

Use this source for:

- practical issue decomposition;
- distinction between finding and using legal sources;
- authority-supported propositions;
- applying law to facts;
- structured practical exercises and reflection;
- preparation before negotiation.

Do not retain:

- dated substantive-law propositions from the 2011 edition;
- student-assessment mechanics as production architecture;
- provider/database details without current verification.

# 5. Source 2 — Allbon / Hunter / Clinch, *Legal Research: A Practitioner’s Handbook*

## 5.1 Material examined

The user-supplied file is a summary only. It was expanded with direct text from the 3rd edition exposed by vLex.

Book-level source:

- `https://vlex.co.uk/source/legal-research-practitioners-handbook-third-edition-42476`

Direct chapter material examined:

- Chapter 3, “How to Choose the Sources”, pp. 21–31  
  `https://vlex.co.uk/vid/how-to-choose-the-976754287`
- Chapter 5, “Legislation”, pp. 47–91  
  `https://vlex.co.uk/vid/legislation-976754294`
- Chapter 6, “Case Law”, pp. 93–120  
  `https://vlex.co.uk/vid/case-law-976754295`
- Chapter 10, “Updating Known Law”, pp. 159–193  
  `https://vlex.co.uk/vid/updating-known-law-976754305`

The book-level table of contents was also used to locate the broader workflow:

```text
problem analysis
→ source selection
→ source evaluation
→ legislation / case law / procedure / precedents / commentary
→ updating known law
→ presentation of results
```

## 5.2 Finding: source selection has three independent quality questions

Chapter 3 requires the researcher to ask whether a legal statement is:

```text
authoritative
accurate
up to date
```

These are not one quality dimension.

A source can be:

- authoritative but stale for a historical/current-law question;
- current but non-authoritative;
- apparently authoritative but inaccurately summarized;
- accurate as commentary but not sufficient evidence for a proposition requiring primary authority.

### Production implication

Legal Skills should not assign one opaque “source confidence” score.

Preserve separate dimensions at minimum:

```text
authority status
source fidelity / accuracy check
temporal currency
```

## 5.3 Finding: prefer full text and triangulate material propositions

The chapter distinguishes full text from summaries and recommends checking material findings against additional sources where possible.

The web-expansion step itself demonstrates why this matters: the user supplied a two-page summary, but direct chapter text exposed substantially more operational detail.

### Production implication

For material conclusions:

```text
summary / search result
→ locate fuller source
→ verify source identity and authority
→ compare against another appropriate source where the claim warrants it
```

Triangulation must not be mistaken for a vote. Two secondary sources repeating the same error do not outrank a controlling primary source.

## 5.4 Finding: known-item search and subject search are different problems

The legislation and case-law chapters distinguish:

```text
known title / citation
from
unknown authorities relevant to a topic
```

For topic research the book warns against immediately throwing broad keywords into an expensive full-text database. It recommends using commentary, practitioner works, indexes or other orientation sources to discover key terminology and authorities, then refining the search using what those sources reveal.

### Production implication

Legal research should choose a search strategy from the information state:

```text
known authority
→ retrieve / verify directly

unknown authority for known issue
→ orient → derive legal terminology → targeted search

unclear issue
→ clarify facts / issue before expensive retrieval
```

This is a strong fit with the Production Skills “cheapest adequate representation” principle.

## 5.5 Finding: “current law” is a bundle of update tasks

Chapter 10 separates at least six update questions:

```text
progress of proposed legislation
whether legislation / treaty is in force
whether it has been amended / repealed / revoked
how courts have interpreted it
progress of a case through courts
whether a court decision remains good law
```

### Production implication

“Current” must not be a single retrieval timestamp.

The legal evidence model should be able to record, where material:

```text
source publication date
effective / commencement status
amendment / repeal / revocation status
case procedural history
later treatment
retrieved-at date
analysis valid-as-of date
```

## 5.6 Finding: the legal researcher should decide whether full text or a summary is adequate

The case-law chapter explicitly asks whether the task needs the full judgment or only a summary.

This reinforces a cost/fidelity rule:

```text
use summary material for orientation when adequate
but
promote to full / authoritative text before relying on a material proposition that needs it
```

The same rule should apply to regulator guidance, legislation and registry evidence.

## 5.7 Finding: case research must include later procedural and precedential status

The case-law chapter directs researchers to verify case progress and whether a decision remains good law. It also distinguishes reported/unreported material and recognizes source hierarchy and citation practice.

### Production implication

A case record used in material analysis should preserve enough state to answer:

```text
which court?
which judgment / report / transcript?
reported or unreported?
what proposition is relied on?
what later procedural history?
what later treatment?
what jurisdiction and date?
```

## 5.8 Finding: presentation is part of research quality

The book devotes a separate part to presenting legal-research results effectively.

That supports an important Legal Skills boundary:

```text
research completed internally
≠
research usable by the next decision-maker
```

The output should expose the question, material facts, authorities, analysis, unresolved issues and actions rather than merely dump citations.

## 5.9 Limitations

The durable method is stronger than the provider-specific detail.

Material that requires Stage 4 or later verification includes:

- database interfaces and product names from 2019;
- post-Brexit UK/EU research routes;
- current court practice directions;
- AI-assisted research provider behaviour;
- current official web portals.

## 5.10 Disposition

**Retain strongly.**

This source provides the corpus's strongest production-specific research method and should materially shape:

- research planning;
- source selection;
- authority verification;
- full-text escalation;
- triangulation;
- currency checks;
- current-law monitoring;
- evidence packaging.

# 6. Source 3 — Schauer, *Thinking Like a Lawyer*

## 6.1 Material examined

Direct full-text examination focused on:

```text
Chapter 1 — Is There Legal Reasoning?
Chapter 2 — Rules—in Law and Elsewhere
Chapter 3 — The Practice and Problems of Precedent
Chapter 4 — Authority and Authorities
Chapter 5 — The Use and Abuse of Analogies
Chapter 7 — The Challenge of Legal Realism
Chapter 8 — The Interpretation of Statutes
Chapter 11 — Law and Fact
Chapter 12 — The Burden of Proof and Its Cousins
```

## 6.2 Finding: legal authority is not the same as persuasive reasoning

Schauer treats authority-based reasoning as distinctive precisely because the source/status of a proposition can make a difference independently of whether the decision-maker finds its underlying reasoning persuasive.

### Production implication

Legal Skills must separate:

```text
binding / controlling force
persuasive force
analytical persuasiveness
policy attractiveness
```

A model must not reject controlling authority merely because another source offers better reasoning, nor elevate persuasive commentary merely because it sounds more convincing.

## 6.3 Finding: rule text and background purpose can diverge

The analysis of rules distinguishes:

```text
what the rule says
from
why the rule exists
```

Rules may be overinclusive or underinclusive relative to their background justification. Legal systems differ in when text yields to purpose.

### Production implication

For material rule application, preserve:

```text
operative text
identified purpose / rationale if legally relevant
permitted interpretive method
application to facts
uncertainty about the relationship between text and purpose
```

Do not silently rewrite a rule to fit the analyst's preferred policy result.

## 6.4 Finding: litigated cases are a biased sample of law in operation

Schauer emphasizes the selection effect: disputed and appellate cases disproportionately concern hard questions at the fringes of rules.

### Production implication

Do not infer that every legal question is indeterminate simply because reported cases disproportionately feature ambiguity and disagreement.

The system should distinguish:

```text
clear core application
from
edge / contested application
```

and allocate research and review depth accordingly.

## 6.5 Finding: precedent requires both hierarchy and relevant similarity

The precedent chapters distinguish vertical and horizontal precedent and show that identifying a relevant precedent requires a judgment about what similarities and differences are legally material.

### Production implication

Case matching cannot be reduced to semantic similarity.

A precedent analysis should preserve:

```text
court hierarchy
precedential status
material facts
legal issue
holding / ratio
relevant similarities
relevant differences
distinguishing / overruling / later-treatment path
```

## 6.6 Finding: analogy is not a substitute for authority analysis

Schauer distinguishes analogical reasoning from precedent. Two matters may be usefully analogous without one legally controlling the other.

### Production implication

Label analogical arguments as analogical. Do not present a similar case as binding merely because its facts resemble the current matter.

## 6.7 Finding: Legal Realism is a challenge to overly formal models of legal reasoning

The Legal Realism chapter asks how much rules and precedent actually explain real outcomes and highlights the possible role of policy, attitudes and other non-doctrinal factors.

### Production implication

Legal Skills should avoid two opposite errors:

```text
formalism error:
“the source mechanically determines the answer”

realist overreach:
“doctrine and authority do not matter”
```

The safe production behaviour is to state what authority constrains, what discretion remains, what facts or judgement drive the remaining choice, and what uncertainty should be escalated.

## 6.8 Finding: facts are a separate epistemic problem

The “Law and Fact” chapter challenges legal analysis that focuses on doctrine while assuming the factual record is reliable.

### Production implication

Every material fact should have a status such as:

```text
verified fact
user-provided assertion
inference
assumption
missing / disputed fact
```

Legal certainty cannot exceed factual certainty.

## 6.9 Finding: burden, presumption and deference allocate uncertainty and decision rights

The final chapter treats burdens of proof, presumptions and deference as mechanisms for deciding what happens when certainty is incomplete and for allocating decision-making responsibility.

A particularly important general principle is:

```text
legal analysis asks not only
“What is the right answer?”

but also
“Whose job is it to decide?”
```

### Production implication

This supports the Stage 1 professional boundary. Legal Skills can analyse and package an issue while still recognizing that the ultimate decision belongs to:

- a court;
- a regulator;
- an authorized professional;
- a registry;
- the client / project owner;
- another designated decision-maker.

## 6.10 Disposition

**Retain strongly, with jurisdiction caution.**

Use Schauer for the reasoning model, especially:

- source authority;
- rules vs purposes;
- precedent;
- analogy;
- doctrinal constraint vs judgement;
- fact uncertainty;
- burdens / presumptions / deference;
- decision-right boundaries.

Do not transplant US doctrinal examples as current UK law.

# 7. Source 4 — Adams, *A Manual of Style for Contract Drafting*

## 7.1 Material examined

Direct full-text examination focused on:

- Introduction / purpose and scope;
- Chapter 1 — Characteristics of Optimal Contract Language;
- Chapter 3 — Categories of Contract Language;
- Chapter 7 — Sources of Uncertain Meaning in Contract Language;
- selected sections on reviewing the other side's draft;
- organisational change, style guides, training, templates and automation;
- Chapter 18 — Amendments.

## 7.2 Finding: contract drafting should be active rather than precedent-faithful by default

Adams contrasts passive drafting—copying precedent because it was used before—with active drafting grounded in explicit drafting principles and controlled templates.

### Production implication

A Legal Skills drafting operation should never treat a precedent as automatically correct merely because it is familiar.

Before reusing a clause:

```text
identify its intended function
verify that it matches the present deal / product / facts
check jurisdiction-specific legal effect where material
check whether the language creates uncertainty
preserve provenance
```

## 7.3 Finding: contract language performs different legal functions

Adams classifies contract language by function, including language of:

```text
agreement / performance
obligation
discretion
prohibition
policy
conditions
declaration / statement of fact
intention / recommendation
```

### Production implication

A drafting system should represent what a provision is meant to **do**, not only what topic it is about.

This supports a clause review such as:

```text
intended legal function
→ actual linguistic function
→ mismatch?
→ smallest responsible correction
```

It also creates testable failure cases: an obligation expressed as a condition is not merely a style defect.

## 7.4 Finding: uncertain meaning has multiple failure modes

The manual separates sources of uncertainty including:

- ambiguity;
- insufficient specificity;
- mistake;
- conflict;
- failure to address an issue;
- vagueness.

Vagueness is not automatically defective; it can be useful when flexibility is intended.

### Production implication

Do not use one generic “unclear clause” label.

Diagnosis should identify the owning failure type because repair differs:

```text
ambiguity → remove alternative meanings
insufficient specificity → add required precision
conflict → reconcile provisions
omission → add missing rule / allocation
mistake → correct the erroneous term / fact / cross-reference
useful vagueness → preserve unless context requires a clearer standard
```

## 7.5 Finding: reviewing the other side's draft is not an excuse to rewrite everything

Adams advises reviewers to focus on provisions that fail to reflect the deal or create meaningful confusion, rather than beautifying harmless drafting merely to impose house style.

### Production implication

This strongly supports the Production Skills principle:

> preserve accepted work and repair the smallest responsible unit.

A redline should distinguish:

```text
material legal / semantic defect
from
harmless stylistic preference
```

## 7.6 Finding: clarity, precision and consistency are operational controls

Adams treats consistency of usage as a way of reducing accidental differences in meaning. He also links clear drafting to lower review cost and lower dispute risk.

### Production implication

A contract-quality check should evaluate dimensions separately:

```text
semantic correctness
legal function
precision
internal consistency
cross-reference integrity
unnecessary redundancy
readability / usability
```

not collapse them into a single prose score.

## 7.7 Finding: organizational quality needs style guides, training and controlled templates

The manual argues that individual skill alone does not scale. Organizations need shared drafting guidance, training and high-quality templates.

### Production implication

Legal Skills should be capable of producing reusable drafting conventions and vetted references, but project-specific templates remain project assets unless they have been generalized and validated.

## 7.8 Finding: automation increases the need for editorial and subject-matter control

The organisational sections discuss automated contract generation as a specialization mechanism when carefully prepared text and expert control underpin the automation.

### Production implication

Automation does not remove legal judgement. It moves judgement into:

```text
questionnaire / input design
approved clause variants
conditions for selecting variants
legal review of reusable text
change control
regression testing
```

## 7.9 Finding: amendment strategy should be proportional to change scope

The amendments chapter distinguishes a small discrete amendment from amending-and-restating a document where the overall text needs replacement.

### Production implication

Use the cheapest representation adequate to the change:

```text
few isolated changes → targeted amendment / redline
broad interconnected change → consider consolidated restatement
```

Preserve unaffected verified language when possible.

## 7.10 Limitations

- primarily business-contract focused;
- not a consumer-contract treatise;
- predominantly US examples, with international drafting observations;
- strong, sometimes intentionally contrarian recommendations need jurisdiction-specific legal verification before being treated as legal effect;
- contract language craft does not by itself establish whether a business term is lawful or fair.

## 7.11 Disposition

**Retain strongly for drafting and review method.**

Do not promote Adams's preferred wording into universal boilerplate. Promote the method:

```text
function
→ meaning
→ uncertainty diagnosis
→ proportional repair
→ verify legal effect
```

The legal-risk chapter exposes substantial direct text, not merely its abstract. That chapter plus the full preface supplies the main direct-source backbone; other official chapter abstracts extend the coverage map without being represented as full chapter examination.

## 8.2 Finding: legal capability belongs inside project decision-making, not at the end of it

The preface presents law as both a constraint and a resource. Its strongest reusable production idea is temporal and organizational:

```text
legal input should enter early enough to influence design and decisions
rather than
arrive after the product / deal is effectively locked
```

The preface explicitly connects proactive legal thinking with early product design and activities throughout a value chain.

### Production implication

Legal Skills should support:

```text
proposed product / feature / deal change
→ early legal scope
→ legal requirements / risks / options
→ project decision
→ implementation
→ later consistency check
```

It should not wait for “generate terms and conditions” as the first legal touchpoint.

## 8.3 Finding: legal astuteness is cross-functional capability

The handbook defines legal astuteness around management and counsel communicating effectively and working together on complex problems. It combines:

- recognition of law's importance;
- proactive regulation/risk posture;
- informed judgement on legal/business aspects;
- context-specific legal knowledge;
- appropriate use of legal tools.

### Production implication

Legal Skills should make legal reasoning understandable and actionable for non-lawyer project contributors without pretending that those contributors become lawyers.

A useful handoff therefore contains:

```text
what changed legally
why it matters to this project
what project behaviour is affected
what options exist
what must not be changed without further review
who must decide / approve
```

## 8.4 Finding: counsel must preserve independent legal judgement

The preface does not endorse legal strategy at any cost. It emphasizes ethical behaviour, independent legal judgement and the danger of subjugating legal imperatives to management preference.

### Production implication

The project must reject this invalid interpretation of proactive law:

```text
“find a clever legal path to whatever the project wants”
```

Instead:

```text
project objective
+ law
+ ethics / professional boundary
+ evidence
→ lawful options and constraints
```

Legal Skills may frame options. It must not manufacture permission.

## 8.5 Finding: legal knowledge should flow across domain boundaries

The knowledge-management chapter treats the client and lawyer as holders of different knowledge domains and emphasizes knowledge co-creation. It also addresses the flow of legal information from legal departments to executives and operational staff.

### Production implication

This strongly supports the family boundary:

```text
consuming project owns project reality
Legal Skills owns reusable legal-production expertise
```

Neither side is sufficient alone.

Legal Skills should request and consume the project facts it needs, return legal conclusions and requirements in an actionable form, and avoid copying the consuming project's full knowledge base into a central legal repository.

## 8.6 Finding: legal communication is itself a control surface

The corporate-communication chapter treats legal communication as behaviour-shaping, not merely information transmission. It highlights legal design, visual methods and prototyping as ways to improve intelligibility and effectiveness.

### Production implication

A legally correct instruction that project contributors cannot understand or operationalize can still fail in production.

Evaluation should distinguish:

```text
legal correctness
from
communication effectiveness
```

## 8.7 Finding: legal risk management should be systematic and cyclical

The legal-risk chapter describes legal risk management as part of broader organizational risk management and presents recurring steps such as:

```text
identify
→ analyze
→ evaluate
→ treat
→ review / monitor
```

It also emphasizes information sharing, a shared risk language and cross-functional participation.

### Production implication

Legal risk is not a one-time memo output. Legal conclusions can become stale when:

- the law changes;
- facts change;
- the product changes;
- controls drift;
- a court or regulator changes interpretation;
- risk treatment changes exposure.

This supports legal change-impact and monitoring capabilities.

## 8.8 Finding: legal and factual uncertainty are distinct risk inputs

The legal-risk chapter explicitly treats uncertainty in the legal norm and uncertainty in the triggering event/facts as separable sources of legal risk.

### Production implication

Do not collapse:

```text
uncertain law
and
uncertain facts
```

into one “medium confidence” label.

They have different repair routes:

```text
uncertain law → better authority / specialist interpretation / wait for development
uncertain fact → investigate / request evidence / preserve assumption
```

## 8.9 Finding: proactive legal design links legal artefacts to real users and operations

The proactive-contracting chapter criticizes legalistic contracts written only for lawyers and argues for multidisciplinary, user-centred contract design that can improve working relationships and prevent misunderstandings.

### Production implication

This complements Adams rather than replacing him:

```text
Adams → semantic precision and function of contract language
Law & Management → usability, organizational fit and operational adoption
```

A high-quality legal artefact should therefore be evaluated both for legal precision and for whether the intended actors can use it correctly.

## 8.10 Finding: systems thinking supports project-to-law traceability

The systems-thinking chapter treats organizations as operating in complex interdependent environments and uses systemic design to reconcile multiple perspectives.

### Production implication

Legal Skills should reason across dependencies such as:

```text
legal obligation
→ product requirement
→ implementation / control
→ user-facing representation
→ evidence / monitoring
```

but should not build a universal enterprise ontology during bootstrap.

## 8.11 Limitation: strategy is adjacent, not core ownership

The handbook deliberately examines competitive and strategic uses of law. `legal-skills` must not therefore absorb product strategy or commercial decision-making.

The safe boundary is:

```text
Legal Skills owns legal implications, constraints, options, risk and legal-tool use.
The consuming project owns whether and how to pursue the commercial / product objective.
```

## 8.12 Disposition

**Retain strongly, with boundary qualification.**

The handbook supplies the corpus's strongest basis for:

- proactive legal involvement;
- legal-product / legal-operation integration;
- legal risk management;
- knowledge transfer;
- legal communication;
- contract usability;
- legal change impact;
- cross-functional legal capability.

Do not treat competitive advantage as overriding legality, ethics, professional independence or the project's explicit non-goals.

# 9. Cross-source reconciliation

The five books do not describe one identical workflow. Their value is complementary.

## 9.1 Matter decomposition precedes expensive research

Supported by:

- Finch & Fafinski — issue decomposition and application;
- Allbon / Hunter / Clinch — problem analysis and source choice before broad searching.

Reconciled rule:

```text
facts / objective
→ identify question and missing facts
→ decompose issues
→ define research need
→ select source strategy
```

Do not begin with unrestricted search merely because search is available.

## 9.2 Source authority and source accuracy are separate from reasoning quality

Supported by:

- Legal Research — authority, accuracy, currency;
- Schauer — authority can operate independently of persuasiveness;
- Legal Skills — binding/persuasive precedent and ratio.

Reconciled rule:

```text
source status
+ source fidelity
+ temporal validity
+ extracted proposition
+ reasoning / applicability
```

must remain inspectable dimensions.

## 9.3 Facts and law need separate evidence chains

Supported by:

- Finch & Fafinski — problem facts drive issue/application;
- Schauer — factual uncertainty is not resolved by doctrinal sophistication;
- Oxford Handbook — legal risk can arise from legal uncertainty and factual uncertainty separately.

Reconciled rule:

```text
fact evidence chain
and
legal authority chain
```

meet at application; neither substitutes for the other.

## 9.4 Drafting is downstream of established meaning

Supported by:

- Finch & Fafinski — question / law / application before conclusion;
- Adams — active drafting and functional categories;
- Oxford Handbook — proactive contract design and organizational usability.

Reconciled rule:

```text
known objective + facts + legal constraints
→ decide intended legal / operational effect
→ draft
→ diagnose semantic / legal / usability failures
→ targeted repair
```

Do not generate polished legal prose first and reverse-engineer the justification afterward.

## 9.5 Legal artefacts are operational controls, not isolated documents

Supported by:

- Adams — contracts regulate conduct, state facts and allocate risk;
- Oxford Handbook — legal communication, legal design, knowledge management and proactive law;
- Stage 1 — privacy notices, terms, licences and disclosures must match actual product behaviour.

Reconciled rule:

```text
legal artefact
↔ actual behaviour / process / control
```

Consistency is a first-class legal-production quality.

## 9.6 Legal uncertainty should drive deeper research or escalation, not confident prose

Supported by:

- Legal Research — source/currency verification;
- Schauer — hard cases, interpretation, burdens and deference;
- Oxford Handbook — systematic legal-risk treatment;
- Adams — uncertainty taxonomy in contract language.

Reconciled response to uncertainty:

```text
identify uncertainty type
→ determine whether more evidence can reduce it
→ research / investigate / redraft where responsible
→ expose residual uncertainty
→ escalate when decision authority or risk requires it
```

## 9.7 Human review is not an embarrassment or fallback

Supported by:

- Schauer — decision authority and deference;
- Oxford Handbook — collaboration between management and legal professionals;
- Stage 1 professional boundary.

Reconciled rule:

A capable system knows when it can produce a bounded legal work product and when another actor has the authority or competence to decide.

## 9.8 Repair should target the owning layer

Supported most directly by Adams, reinforced by the corpus's separate treatment of:

- facts;
- source retrieval;
- source authority;
- reasoning;
- language;
- operational implementation.

Example:

```text
wrong fact
→ repair fact and dependent application

stale authority
→ update authority and dependent proposition / conclusion

ambiguous clause
→ repair clause, not entire agreement

product behaviour drift
→ repair implementation and affected legal artefact / analysis
```

# 10. Material tensions and qualifications

## 10.1 Authority versus instrumental use of law

**Tension:**

Schauer emphasizes that legal authority can constrain even when the decision-maker dislikes the result. The Law & Management literature emphasizes strategic and instrumental uses of law.

**Resolution:**

Strategic legal capability operates **within** the applicable authority structure. It can identify lawful options, design better controls and use legal tools proactively. It cannot treat binding law as optional because another outcome would be strategically attractive.

**Disposition:** retain both; make the boundary explicit.

## 10.2 Student problem-solving model versus professional legal production

**Tension:**

Finch & Fafinski's issue-law-application-conclusion structure is intentionally teachable and compact. Professional matters may involve multi-jurisdictional source conflict, evidence uncertainty, risk trade-offs and specialist review.

**Resolution:**

Retain issue decomposition and application as the reasoning kernel. Extend the production model with jurisdiction, date, authority, factual status, uncertainty, project action and review.

**Disposition:** adapt.

## 10.3 Provider-specific research mechanics versus durable research method

**Tension:**

The 2019 practitioner handbook contains detailed database/provider mechanics that change faster than its core methodology.

**Resolution:**

Retain:

```text
research question
source type
known-item vs subject search
authority / accuracy / currency
full-text escalation
updating / later treatment
```

Verify provider-specific execution at runtime or in later tool research.

**Disposition:** retain method, re-verify implementations.

## 10.4 Adams's drafting preferences versus jurisdiction-specific legal effect

**Tension:**

Adams intentionally challenges conventional drafting and draws heavily from US business-contract practice.

**Resolution:**

Use his functional and uncertainty-diagnosis method. Do not turn preferred phrases into universal boilerplate without jurisdiction- and matter-specific validation.

**Disposition:** adapt.

## 10.5 Precision versus usability

**Tension:**

Adams prioritizes semantic precision and consistency. Legal design emphasizes readability, usability and cross-functional adoption.

**Resolution:**

These are different quality dimensions, not mutually exclusive objectives.

A legal artefact can fail because it is:

- semantically imprecise;
- legally wrong;
- operationally unusable;
- inconsistent with actual behaviour.

**Disposition:** retain both; evaluate separately.

## 10.6 Legal strategy versus project ownership

**Tension:**

Law & Management includes competitive legal strategy. The Production Skills family requires the consuming project to own its strategy and requirements.

**Resolution:**

Legal Skills supplies legal options, constraints, consequences, risk and legal-tool implications. The consuming project decides the commercial or product path.

**Disposition:** boundary-qualified retain.

# 11. Provisional legal-production capability model

This is a capability model, **not a skill list**.

No fixed skill count is justified at Stage 3.

## C1 — Matter framing and fact control

Inputs:

```text
project request
known facts
available evidence
stated objective
```

Behaviour:

- identify actors, events and material facts;
- separate fact / assertion / inference / assumption / unknown;
- identify missing information;
- frame the legal question at an appropriate level.

Output:

```text
matter brief
fact / assumption inventory
open questions
```

Primary source contributions:

- Finch & Fafinski;
- Schauer;
- Oxford Handbook.

## C2 — Jurisdiction, time and decision-right framing

Behaviour:

- identify potentially relevant jurisdiction / territory;
- identify relevant matter date and analysis valid-as-of date;
- identify whose decision or approval is legally/professionally required;
- distinguish what Legal Skills may analyse from what another actor must decide.

Primary source contributions:

- Schauer;
- Legal Research;
- Stage 1 boundary.

## C3 — Issue decomposition and research planning

Behaviour:

```text
matter
→ issues / subissues
→ proposition questions
→ evidence needs
→ source / search strategy
```

Primary source contributions:

- Finch & Fafinski;
- Legal Research.

## C4 — Authority retrieval, verification and currency control

Behaviour:

- choose source type deliberately;
- distinguish known-item and subject research;
- prefer authoritative/full sources for material propositions;
- verify authority, accuracy and currency;
- trace amendments, commencement, repeal, case progress and later treatment;
- use secondary sources for orientation without promoting them automatically to authority.

Primary source contribution:

- Legal Research, reinforced by Finch & Fafinski and Schauer.

## C5 — Proposition extraction and legal applicability reasoning

Behaviour:

- state the proposition independently;
- identify supporting source location;
- distinguish binding, persuasive and merely informative material;
- identify material facts / conditions;
- apply rules, precedent, analogy and permitted interpretive methods;
- record counterarguments and edge cases;
- distinguish clear-core application from contested/fringe application.

Primary source contributions:

- Schauer;
- Finch & Fafinski;
- Legal Research.

## C6 — Legal risk, option and uncertainty framing

Behaviour:

- distinguish factual, authority, temporal, jurisdictional and interpretive uncertainty;
- identify consequence / exposure where matter-specific evidence permits;
- frame lawful options and risk-treatment choices;
- identify what additional evidence can reduce uncertainty;
- avoid replacing uncertainty with model confidence.

Primary source contributions:

- Oxford Handbook;
- Schauer;
- Legal Research.

## C7 — Legal drafting, review and redlining

Behaviour:

- start from established objective / facts / legal constraints;
- identify intended function of provisions;
- diagnose ambiguity, insufficient specificity, conflict, omission, mistake and useful/defective vagueness;
- preserve acceptable language;
- make the smallest responsible correction;
- verify the legal effect of nontrivial drafting choices;
- distinguish semantic clarity from substantive legality/fairness.

Primary source contribution:

- Adams, complemented by Oxford Handbook legal design.

## C8 — Legal-product consistency and change impact

Behaviour:

- map legal propositions / obligations to project requirements, controls and artefacts;
- detect inconsistency between legal representations and actual behaviour;
- identify dependent legal work when law, facts or product behaviour changes;
- support monitoring / review loops for continuing obligations.

Primary source contribution:

- Oxford Handbook, reinforced by Adams and Stage 1.

## C9 — Legal communication and evidence packaging

Behaviour:

- communicate legal findings at the fidelity required by the consumer;
- preserve source and reasoning traceability;
- make uncertainty and decision points visible;
- translate legal requirements into language usable by product / engineering / creative contributors without silently changing legal meaning.

Primary source contributions:

- Oxford Handbook;
- Legal Research;
- Finch & Fafinski.

## C10 — Specialist escalation and bounded human review

Behaviour:

When competence, authority, reserved activity or risk requires another actor, produce:

```text
precise question
material facts / assumptions
jurisdiction / date
authorities examined
propositions / application
conflicts / uncertainty
available options
specific decision / review requested
```

Primary source contributions:

- Schauer's allocation of decision responsibility;
- Oxford Handbook's lawyer/management collaboration;
- Stage 1 professional boundary.

# 14. Provisional quality dimensions derived from the corpus

The corpus reinforces Stage 1's decision not to use one legal-quality score.

At minimum, later evaluation should distinguish:

1. **matter framing** — were the right question and material facts identified?
2. **source authority** — are the sources appropriate to the proposition?
3. **source fidelity** — was the source read correctly rather than inferred from a summary?
4. **temporal validity** — was the relevant law/guidance valid for the matter date?
5. **jurisdiction fit** — is the proposition being applied in the correct legal context?
6. **reasoning quality** — are rule, precedent, analogy, interpretation and application handled correctly?
7. **factual epistemics** — are fact, assumption and inference separated?
8. **uncertainty calibration** — are unresolved legal/factual questions visible?
9. **drafting semantics** — does legal language perform its intended function without avoidable uncertainty?
10. **project consistency** — do legal artefacts and project behaviour agree?
11. **communication usability** — can the next actor understand and execute the legal requirement?
12. **professional boundary** — are specialist and reserved decisions escalated appropriately?
13. **repair discipline** — is correction scoped to the failing evidence / reasoning / clause / implementation layer?

# 15. Anti-patterns exposed by the corpus

The extraction provides concrete anti-patterns for later benchmarks.

## 15.1 Search-first lawyering

```text
vague request
→ broad web/database search
→ answer from first plausible result
```

Failure: issue and source strategy were never established.

## 15.2 Citation laundering

```text
model summary
→ citation attached
→ claim treated as authority
```

Failure: source authority, source text and extracted proposition were never checked.

## 15.3 Summary-to-rule promotion

```text
headnote / snippet / article
→ substantive legal proposition
```

without locating an adequate full / authoritative source where the proposition requires one.

## 15.4 Authority by persuasiveness

```text
preferred commentary sounds convincing
→ controlling authority ignored
```

Failure: analytical persuasiveness was confused with legal authority.

## 15.5 Case similarity without precedent analysis

```text
embedding similarity
→ “this case controls”
```

Failure: hierarchy, ratio and material factual differences were not examined.

## 15.6 False factual certainty

```text
user statement
→ silently treated as verified fact
→ categorical legal answer
```

Failure: law/fact evidence chains collapsed.

## 15.7 Template-first drafting

```text
find familiar clause
→ paste
→ cosmetically edit
```

Failure: provision function, current matter and legal effect were not established.

## 15.8 Full-contract rewrite for local defect

Failure: safe and accepted language is disturbed unnecessarily.

## 15.9 “Clear English” as substitute for legality

A clause can be readable and still unlawful, unfair or operationally impossible.

## 15.10 Legal memo disconnected from product reality

Failure: privacy/terms/licence/AI disclosures describe a system that does not exist.

## 15.11 Legal strategy without ethical / authority constraint

Failure: proactive law is misused as permission to circumvent legal obligations.

## 15.12 Escalation by generic disclaimer

```text
“consult a lawyer”
```

without specifying the unresolved issue, facts, authority, uncertainty and decision required.

# 17. Claims deliberately not promoted from the corpus

The following are not accepted as unconditional core rules at Stage 3:

- that legal strategy always creates competitive advantage;
- that every legal matter benefits from formal risk quantification;
- that a specific contract wording is universally optimal;
- that a particular 2019 database/provider remains the best current research route;
- that the 2011 account of UK/EU law remains current;
- that every legal question follows one four-step reasoning formula;
- that formal legal authority mechanically determines every difficult outcome;
- that legal design always improves legal outcomes;
- that more cross-functional involvement is always better regardless of privilege, confidentiality or decision rights;
- that AI can autonomously perform work reserved to authorized legal professionals.

These claims require broader challenge evidence, jurisdiction-specific qualification or rejection.
