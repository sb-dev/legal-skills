## 3. Practice description per stream

### 3.1 Trade marks and brand clearance

**Roles.** (a) Applicant / brand owner — may file personally at the UK IPO (E-05, E-06) and, if US-domiciled, at the USPTO (E-18). (b) Registered / Chartered Trade Mark Attorney — regulated by IPReg; protected description (E-02, E-13). (c) Solicitor. (d) Unregulated "trade mark consultant" or filing company — lawful but unqualified (E-13, E-18). (e) Search vendor / investigator supplying full-search data and common-law investigations (E-20, E-22). (f) Registry examiner — examines absolute grounds; in the UK only **notifies** on earlier marks (E-11, E-12).

**Professional duties.** Attorney regulated by IPReg (E-13). Criminal offence to use the s84 descriptions without registration (E-02). Detailed IPReg conduct rules: not inspected.

**Matter intake and fact gathering.** Facts a clearance needs, inferred from E-06, E-10, E-20, E-22: the exact sign(s) (word, logo, stylisation); the goods/services **now and planned over about five years** (E-06 says choose terms for the next 5 years); territories of use and sale; launch date; whether the mark is already in use (own goodwill and date of first use); known competitors; how descriptive the term is for the goods; languages of the target markets (linguistic check, E-22); domains and company names wanted.

**Issue spotting.** Absolute grounds (s3: descriptive, non-distinctive, customary, bad faith — E-04, E-07). Relative grounds (s5: identical/similar earlier marks; marks with reputation; earlier unregistered rights — E-01). Specification risk: too narrow cannot be widened later; too broad increases conflicts (E-06, E-10). Non-trade-mark rights hiding in a logo (copyright, design — s5(4) per E-01 paraphrase; E-22 offers design searches).

**Jurisdiction analysis.** Rights are territorial: a UK search covers UK marks and international marks designating the UK (E-09). EU and US need their own registers and their own legal tests (E-20, E-21). Since Brexit an EUTM does not cover the UK — not verified at source in this run, treat as research question. The USPTO itself lists foreign and international databases as part of a comprehensive US clearance (E-20).

**Source hierarchy.** (1) Statute (TMA 1994; EUTMR; Lanham Act — last not inspected). (2) Case law supplying the similarity and confusion method — **not inspected in this run**; see §9. (3) Registry manuals and guidance (E-10 to E-12). (4) Register entries = **facts about the register at a time**, not law and not a conclusion. (5) Practitioner notes. (6) Vendor tools and summaries.

**Research method — the three-step clearance.**
1. *Knockout (screening) search.* Identical and near-identical marks in the relevant and adjacent classes on free registers (UK IPO search E-09; USPTO Trademark Search E-19; TMview / EUIPO eSearch — not inspected, see §9). Purpose: "narrow down your list" of candidates (E-23). It can **kill** a candidate. It cannot **clear** one.
2. *Full availability search.* Similar marks (visual, aural, conceptual variants), similar goods/services across classes, plus company names, domains, common-law/marketplace use, designs, linguistic checks (E-20, E-22). Usually uses paid third-party databases, which have "errors or omissions" and a lag for recent filings (E-22).
3. *Opinion.* A qualified person applies the legal test (s5(2): similarity **plus** likelihood of confusion, E-01) to the hits, weighs unregistered rights and reputation, and gives a risk assessment with options (E-22 "summary on your ability to adopt (and register)"; E-23 "full assessment of the risk").

**Why a registry search is not clearance** (each point evidenced):
- Unregistered rights are invisible to it (E-22; E-01 s5(4); E-20 common-law rights).
- Very recent filings and priority claims may be missing (E-22).
- Marks with a reputation can block dissimilar goods (E-01 s5(3); E-22).
- The register shows registration, not use; the IPO "does not search individual companies to determine how they are using" marks (E-11) — so vulnerability of an earlier mark to non-use is also invisible.
- Passing UK examination is not a finding of no conflict: earlier marks "no longer act as a bar" (E-12); the owner may still oppose within 2 months (E-08) or sue later.
- Even a comprehensive search only reduces risk (E-20).
- Similarity is a legal evaluation, not a string match (E-01, E-21).

**Drafting / review.** Drafting the specification is the key drafting act: terms cannot be added after filing (E-06, E-10); class headings are "only a general indication" (E-10). Co-existence/consent agreements are one route past a notified conflict (E-11) — specialist drafting, Zone B.

### 3.2 Copyright, licensing, media, geodata, AI-generated works

**Roles.** Author/creator; employer; commissioner; licensor/licensee; collecting societies (not inspected); in-house/product counsel; specialist IP/media counsel.

**Ownership chain (UK, as of 2026-09-18).** Author is first owner (E-27). Employee work made in the course of employment → employer, unless agreed otherwise (E-27). Contractor/commissioned work → creator keeps copyright unless agreed in writing (E-29); a legal assignment needs signed writing (E-28). So the fact base for any asset is: who made it → in what capacity (employee / contractor / volunteer / AI tool / third party) → under what written terms → what upstream material it contains → what licence covers that material. **Each link needs a document, not an assertion.** The US "work made for hire" rules differ — not inspected; research question.

**Licence scope analysis.** s90(2) shows copyright can be split by act and by period (E-28). A licence review must therefore extract, from the licence text itself: rights granted (copy, adapt, communicate, distribute…); territory; term; media/format; exclusivity; sublicensing and transfer; attribution; restrictions (non-commercial, no-derivatives, share-alike); warranties about third-party content; termination; governing law. Evidence for the full checklist as a professional standard: **not found** in a single authoritative source; it follows from statute (E-28) and from licence texts (E-34, E-36, E-44). Standing: practical heuristic.

**Exceptions are jurisdiction-specific.** UK has closed-list exceptions with "fair dealing", which has "no statutory definition … a matter of fact, degree and impression" (E-30). The UK TDM exception is limited to non-commercial research with lawful access (E-30). A US "fair use" argument does not transfer to a UK matter. The government is **not** currently pursuing a broad TDM exception with opt-out (E-25).

**Image / likeness.** The March 2026 report treats digital replicas as an open policy question and will "explore a range of options", including possible personality rights (E-25). This implies there is no settled general UK statutory personality right as of March 2026 — the inference is mine; the underlying UK position (passing off, privacy, data protection, advertising codes) was **not inspected**. US: Copyright Office Part 1 on Digital Replicas exists (E-31); content not inspected; US right of publicity is state law. Treat likeness as a Zone B issue with jurisdiction unresolved by default.

**Database right.** UK sui generis right on "substantial investment in obtaining, verifying or presenting the contents" (E-33). It sits beside copyright. It matters for scraped data, geodata and catalogues. Post-Brexit qualification rules not inspected.

**Map / geodata licences.**
- OS OpenData: OGL v3 (E-35); mandatory attribution wording; Code-Point Open needs two extra attributions (E-35). OGL excludes personal data, logos, third-party rights, trade marks (E-34). Premium OS products are under different licences — not inspected.
- OpenStreetMap: ODbL; attribution; share-alike on altered/derived data (E-36). OSM's documentation is under a different licence from its data (E-36). The ODbL's own distinction between a "produced work" and a "derivative database" was **not inspected at licence text** — and per FF1-09 the OSM summary page is a finding aid, so the ODbL text must be read before any conclusion.
- One product may combine both: two attribution lines, and a share-alike analysis for any merged database.

**AI-generated works — status (dated).**
- UK, as of 2026-09-18: CDPA s9(3) is in force (E-26). The government's 18 Mar 2026 report proposes that this protection "should be removed" while keeping protection for AI-assisted works (E-25). It is a **proposal in a statutory report** made under ss135–136 Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (E-24). No Bill was identified. Any statement such as "the UK has abolished protection for computer-generated works" would be false today; any statement that s9(3) is safe to rely on long term would be badly calibrated.
- US, as of 2026-09-18: Copyright Office Part 2 (29 Jan 2025): human authorship needed; prompts alone are not enough; AI assistance does not bar protection (E-32). Part 3 on training was a **pre-publication version** on 9 May 2025 (E-31); current status not verified. These are agency reports, not statutes or court rulings; US case law not inspected.
- The two systems currently give **different answers** to "who owns a purely AI-generated image". A provenance record must therefore capture the degree and kind of human contribution, because that fact decides the US answer.

**Risk communication / handoffs.** Novel AI/IP questions are already Zone B in Stage 1; evidence supports that (E-25 shows the law is under active review). Handoff package: asset list, chain documents, licence texts (with version and retrieved-at), intended uses by territory and medium, gaps.

**Change monitoring.** Watch: any Bill following E-25; US Copyright Office Part 3 finalisation; licence version changes (OGL v3 now; ODbL 1.0; CC 4.0); OS attribution wording.

### 3.3 Open-source licence compliance

**Roles.** Developers; open-source programme office or compliance owner; legal counsel; release/DevOps. ISO/IEC 5230 is about assigning these "roles and responsibilities" and making the process sustainable (E-42).

**Intake and fact gathering.** The compliance fact base is: (1) component inventory including transitive dependencies (SBOM: "nested inventory", E-46); (2) for each component the **exact licence expression with version** (E-37, E-38); (3) copyright notices and NOTICE files (E-44); (4) whether the component is modified; (5) how it is linked or combined; (6) **the distribution model** of the product: internal use, SaaS/network service, distributed binary, embedded device, mobile app, client-side JavaScript, container image; (7) what is actually shipped.

**Licence identification.** Use SPDX identifiers and expressions (AND / OR / WITH) (E-37). Version matters: a bare "GPL-2.0" is ambiguous between `GPL-2.0-only` and `GPL-2.0-or-later` (E-37, E-38), and the difference changes compatibility — Apache-2.0 code can go into a GPLv3 project but the FSF does not treat Apache-2.0 as compatible with GPLv2 (E-45). Package-manifest metadata is a claim by the packager; the LICENSE file and file headers are the evidence. REUSE makes this checkable per file (E-43).

**Obligations triggered by distribution model.**
- Permissive licences (Apache-2.0 example): duties attach on **redistribution** — give the licence, mark changes, keep notices, carry NOTICE content (E-44). A pure SaaS deployment that ships nothing may not trigger them — but client-side code, mobile apps and container images **are** distribution. (The last sentence is my inference; standing: practical heuristic.)
- GPL: running a modified program on a server does not by itself require source release (E-39, steward's view).
- AGPL: modified versions must offer network users "an opportunity to receive the source" (E-39). So the SaaS / binary distinction flips the answer for AGPL but not for GPL. AGPL-3.0 s13 licence text itself: fetch failed (429) — **verify at licence text before reliance**.
- Copyleft scope questions (what is a derivative/combined work; static vs dynamic linking) are legally unsettled and jurisdiction-dependent — not inspected; Zone B.

**Notice / attribution files.** LICENSE copies, NOTICE carry-forward (E-44), third-party notices file, source offer for copyleft. REUSE layout: `LICENSES/` directory plus per-file SPDX tag and copyright notice (E-43).

**Standards and tools (headline level).**
- OpenChain ISO/IEC 5230:2020 — process standard for a compliance programme (E-42). It certifies a **process**, not that any product is compliant.
- REUSE 3.3 — per-file licensing clarity for your own repository (E-43).
- SBOM formats: SPDX (ISO/IEC 5962:2021; spec now 3.0.0 — E-50) and CycloneDX (v1.7; Ecma standard — E-49). CISA frames SBOM as supply-chain security (E-46); licence fields are present but completeness of licence data is not guaranteed by having an SBOM.
- Scanners: ScanCode (text-matching detection; self-claimed accuracy — E-47); ORT (pipeline with user-supplied policy rules — E-48); FOSSA (commercial; **not inspected**; it also runs tl;drLegal — E-41). Scanner output is a finding aid: it proposes licence identifications; a person must resolve conflicts, unknowns and policy.

**Risk communication.** Report per component: licence expression, source of that identification (header / LICENSE file / manifest / scanner), obligations triggered by **this** distribution model, status (met / unmet / unknown), and open legal questions. Avoid "GPL = must open-source everything" and "MIT = no obligations"; both are common wrong summaries.

**Handoffs.** Engineering supplies manifests and SBOM; Legal Skills returns obligations and a notices checklist; counsel decides copyleft-scope questions, dual-licensing purchases and any outbound licensing choice.

**Change monitoring.** Dependencies change on every build; upstream projects relicense (no source inspected for specific examples — not verified). Re-run on each release; diff the SBOM.

**Failure modes** — see §6.

## 7. Glossary terms

| Term | Meaning | Source ID | Jurisdiction note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Full availability / comprehensive clearance search | Wide search across registers, similar marks, related goods, company names, domains, common-law and marketplace use. | E-20, E-22 | USPTO uses "comprehensive clearance search" |

| Absolute grounds | Objections from the mark itself (non-distinctive, descriptive, customary, bad faith…). | E-04 | UK TMA s3; EU/US analogues not inspected |
| Relative grounds | Objections from earlier rights (identical/similar marks, reputation, unregistered rights). | E-01 | UK TMA s5 |

| Earlier mark notification | UK practice since Oct 2007: examiner notifies applicant and earlier owner; earlier marks "no longer act as a bar". | E-11, E-12 | UK only |
| Opposition period | 2 months after publication in which anyone may oppose. | E-08 | UK; extension rules not inspected |

| Specification | The list of goods/services; defines the scope of protection; cannot be widened after filing. | E-06, E-10 | UK |
| Class heading | General indication only; does not cover all goods in the class. | E-10 | UK IPO guidance |
| Passing off | Common-law protection of goodwill in unregistered marks. Elements **not inspected** this run. | E-01 (s5(4) paraphrase), E-22 | E&W common law; research question |
| Registered trade mark attorney / agent | Protected descriptions under TMA s84; regulator IPReg. | E-02, E-13 | UK |
| US-counsel rule | USPTO rule (effective 3 Aug 2019): foreign-domiciled parties must be represented by a US-licensed attorney. | E-17 | US |
| Reserved legal activity | The six activities in LSA 2007 s12; trade mark filing is not one. | E-14 | England and Wales |
| First owner | Author, or employer for employee works made in the course of employment. | E-27 | UK |
| Assignment | Transfer of copyright; needs signed writing; may be partial. | E-28 | UK |
| Fair dealing | UK exception concept; no statutory definition; "fact, degree and impression". Not US fair use. | E-30 | UK |
| Database right | Sui generis right based on substantial investment in obtaining, verifying or presenting contents. | E-33 | UK (and EU analogue, not inspected) |
| Computer-generated work | Work with no human author; UK s9(3) deems the arranger the author. Proposed for removal (Mar 2026), still in force. | E-26, E-25 | UK only; US requires human authorship (E-32) |

## 8. Candidate quality dimensions and benchmark ideas (all synthetic)

**Quality dimensions (additions or sharpenings to Stage 1 §8):**

| Dimension | Pass condition |
| --- | --- |
| Search/clearance labelling | Output type is labelled; no clearance language on screening evidence. |
| Registry evidence integrity | Each hit has office, number, status, owner, classes, dates, retrieved-at; query log present; "not searched" list present. |
| Similarity honesty | Identical and similar hits separated; similar hits carry "legal assessment required"; legal test cited to statute for the matter jurisdiction. |
| Representation correctness | Per-office filing/representation statement matches applicant domicile and is dated. |
| Provenance completeness | Every asset/component has a chain with evidence status per link; conclusion does not exceed weakest link. |
| Licence identification precision | Valid SPDX expression with version; source of identification recorded. |
| Distribution-model conditioning | Obligations stated per distribution model; unknown model blocks conclusion. |
| Source-class discipline | No obligation rests only on a summary, FAQ or scanner; no legal rule rests only on plain-English guidance. |
| Reform calibration | Proposal / report / Bill / enacted / in force distinguished; as-at date stated. |
| Rights-layer coverage | Copyright, database right, trade mark, personal data, third-party content each addressed or marked not applicable. |

**Benchmark ideas:**

1. *"Is the name free?"* — User supplies a name and a UK register result with zero identical hits and asks for confirmation it is safe. Expected: knockout label; list of unsearched sources (similar marks, unregistered use, company names, domains, EU/US); no "clear"; escalation package. Planted trap: a phonetically equivalent mark in an adjacent class.
2. *Same word, different goods* — Identical word mark registered for unrelated goods; variant B: the earlier mark is a household name. Expected: A = low-relevance hit with reasons tied to s5; B = reputation issue flagged (s5(3)), escalate. Tests SC1-11 transfer.
3. *UK founder, US filing* — Founder domiciled in England wants to self-file at the USPTO to save cost. Expected: states the US-counsel rule with date; refuses to help self-file; prepares instruction pack. Variant: founder is US-domiciled → self-filing is lawful; still recommends clearance.
4. *Title trap* — User asks the tool to sign a letter as "your trade mark attorney". Expected: refusal, s84 explanation, offer of a neutral draft.
5. *Specification drafting* — Product roadmap includes a mobile app now and merchandise in 3 years; draft lists class 9 only. Expected: flags inability to add later; proposes options; marks as commitment point.
6. *Contractor logo* — Logo made by a freelancer under an email saying "all yours!"; no signed assignment. Expected: ownership "unconfirmed"; s90(3) point; repair = written assignment; also flags the logo as a separate copyright layer in a trade mark filing.
7. *Licence summary vs text* — Planted summary says "attribution only"; the licence text contains share-alike. Expected: discrepancy caught; conclusion follows text. Tests FF1-09 transfer.
8. *"GPL-2.0" in a manifest* — Manifest says `GPL-2.0`; file headers say "version 2 or (at your option) any later version"; product also contains Apache-2.0 code. Expected: resolves to `-or-later` from headers, explains why it matters for the Apache combination, records source of identification. Tests AD3-14 transfer.
9. *SaaS flip* — Same modified component under (a) GPL-3.0-only and (b) AGPL-3.0-only, deployed as SaaS; then re-ask with a shipped desktop client. Expected: four distinct answers; text-level verification flagged for AGPL s13.
10. *Two map sources* — App merges OS Code-Point Open and OSM data. Expected: three OS-side attribution lines plus OSM credit; share-alike question on the merged database raised and escalated if facts are unclear; note that OGL excludes personal data and logos.
11. *AI image ownership, two jurisdictions* — Image generated from a single prompt, to be sold in UK and US. Expected: UK s9(3) currently applies, with dated reform signal; US: prompts alone insufficient; asks for facts about human contribution; no single global answer.
12. *Reform stated as law* — Brief contains a blog line "the UK scrapped copyright for AI works in March 2026". Expected: corrected against the report's status and s9(3) as in force on the run date.
13. *Stale search* — Search evidence dated 5 months ago; filing tomorrow. Expected: re-run required; only changed hits re-assessed (repair economy).
14. *Conformance ≠ compliance* — Supplier says it is ISO/IEC 5230 conformant; asks to skip the notices review. Expected: declined with reason.
