## 3. Practice description per stream

### 3.1 Consumer / digital commerce (UK first)

**Roles.** In-house product/commercial counsel; external consumer-regulatory lawyers; compliance and CX/UX teams; customer-support operations; billing/engineering; for enforcement: CMA, Trading Standards, sector regulators and Which? (D-01 fn 8). Reed Smith's preparation list spans legal, billing, support scripts and third parties such as app stores (D-35) — the work is cross-functional, not a document exercise.

**Professional duties.** The trader carries the duties: terms must be fair (CRA s62) and transparent (s68) (D-03); practices must not be unfair (DMCC s225) (D-06); pre-contract information must be given "in a clear and comprehensible manner" (D-01 ¶1.13; D-11). A business deploying an AI agent is responsible for it "in the same way you are responsible for what an employee does" (D-30).

**Matter intake and fact gathering.** The fact base is the **actual consumer journey**, not only the terms document. CMA37 requires surprising or onerous terms to be "presented during the consumer journey, as part of the consumer's decision-making process" (D-01 ¶4.42), consistent with "marketing and sales claims during the sales process" (¶6.7). Practitioners advise mapping every product and "where 'renewal events' occur", testing disclosure, reminder and cancellation flows per channel, and keeping "an audit trail of what consumers saw, when, and on which channel/device" (D-35). Mayer Brown: "Audit consumer journeys" for hidden charges and default opt-ins (D-36). Facts to collect: screenshots/recordings of sign-up, checkout, renewal and cancellation on each device; marketing claims; price build-up; default settings; timers; reminder emails; support scripts; AI-agent behaviour; app-store cancellation route.

**Issue spotting.** A layered sequence emerges from D-01/D-03/D-06/D-11:
1. Is it a consumer contract / consumer notice? (D-01 ch 2)
2. Blacklisted terms (CRA Part 1 rights cannot be excluded) (D-01 ch 3).
3. Grey List match (20 paragraphs, indicative and non-exhaustive) (D-04; D-01 ¶2.27).
4. General fairness test — cumulative: significant imbalance **and** contrary to good faith (D-01 ¶4.1).
5. Transparency as a **standalone** duty, enforceable even where the term is not unfair (D-01 ¶1.10, ¶2.30).
6. Core exemption only if transparent **and prominent**; never for Grey List-type terms; labels do not decide (D-01 ¶5.3–5.5).
7. Practices layer: misleading actions/omissions, aggressive practices, drip pricing / invitation to purchase, Sch 20 (D-06); OCA taxonomy (D-10).
8. Pre-contract information, cancellation and refund mechanics (D-11).
9. Forthcoming subscription duties (not in force) (D-05, D-07).
10. Sector overlay: "compliance with sectoral rules does not replace compliance with Part 2" and vice versa (D-01 fn 6).

**Jurisdiction analysis.** CRA Part 2 applies across the UK to contracts from 1 Oct 2015 (D-01 ¶1.1). EU-facing offers bring in the UCTD (minimum harmonisation, so member states may be stricter — D-37 Art 8), the CRD as amended (withdrawal function from 19 Jun 2026 — D-39) and, for online platforms only, DSA Art 25 (D-40). UK guidance still relies heavily on CJEU case law (Kásler, RWE, Andriciuc, Aziz cited throughout D-01), so UK and EU transparency doctrine remain close as of July 2026; divergence is a change-monitoring item, not an assumption.

**Source hierarchy (observed).** Statute and SIs → UK appellate cases (First National Bank, Abbey National, ParkingEye v Beavis, Eternity Sky, Parker-Grennan) → CJEU case law as used by UK courts and the CMA → CMA guidance (CMA37, CMA207, CMA200) → CMA case outcomes and blogs → behavioural research cited by the CMA (D-34; OFT 1312) → practitioner notes (D-35, D-36). CMA37 states of itself: "not a substitute for, or definitive interpretation of, the law" (D-01 ¶1.4) yet "The CMA will have regard to this guidance when it exercises its direct consumer enforcement powers" (¶1.5).

**Research method.** Check commencement at provision level: DMCC Pt 4 Ch 1 commenced 6 Apr 2025 (D-06) but Ch 2 is prospective (D-05), with government anticipating "spring 2027" (D-07). Law-firm notes from 2025 that gave "spring 2026" or "autumn 2026" are now wrong — search results surfaced such titles. Legislation.gov.uk banners show unapplied amendments on CRA, CCRs and FSMA (D-03, D-11, D-12).

**Drafting / review.** What the regulator asks for (D-01 ¶4.33–4.49): everyday words; explain jargon such as "indemnity" and "force majeure"; explain rather than merely cite statutory rights; short sentences; descriptive headings; avoid dense cross-references and "elaborate definitions"; give the **reason** for a term and signpost related terms (¶4.39); emphasise important, onerous or unusual terms — but not everything (¶4.43); use summaries/FAQs that stay consistent with the full terms (¶4.42); keep the full terms available in one place; ensure device-independent readability and accessibility. Do not use "I have read and understood" declarations (¶6.6). Evidence-based presentation techniques exist (D-34). Substantive review asks: would the consumer be surprised; would their lawyer press for deletion (¶4.12); does the term go further than needed for a legitimate interest (¶4.18–4.20); is the effect, not the intention, fair (¶4.20).

**Filing / registration boundaries.** None for consumer terms. Boundary instead: sector overlays (financial services, telecoms) and the point where a CMA information notice or investigation arrives — that is Zone B (specialist) work; procedural fines are real (Euro Car Parks, D-36).

**Risk communication.** Two distinct consequences must be kept apart: (a) private-law — an unfair term "is not binding on the consumer", courts must consider fairness of their own motion (s71, D-03), money may have to be repaid (D-01 ¶1.12); (b) public enforcement — penalties "up to 10% of…global turnover or £300,000 (whichever is greater)" (D-01 ¶1.11). First-year record: £4.7m fines, £760k refunds, 157 letters (D-08).

**Handoffs.** Legal → UX/design (prominence, journey placement, timers, defaults); → billing (refund calculations, renewal events); → support (scripts; AI-agent guardrails, D-30); → marketing (claims consistent with terms, D-01 fn 204).

**Change monitoring.** Active items as of 19 Sep 2026: subscription regime SIs and CMA guidance still to come (D-35); CMA37 "may be revised from time to time" to reflect enforcement (D-01 ¶1.4); reference-pricing issue unresolved in Emma (D-32); EU CRD withdrawal-function transposition (D-39).

**Failure modes and repairs.** See §6.

### 3.2 Financial-services / fintech perimeter (issue spotting only)

**Roles.** Financial-regulatory specialists (external or in-house), compliance officers, the firm's senior managers; the FCA's Innovation Pathways and pre-application support. Generalist product counsel and tools spot and package; they do not conclude.

**Duties and stakes.** The general prohibition (s19, D-12) is backed by a criminal offence with up to two years' imprisonment on indictment and a due-diligence defence (s23, D-14). s21 separately restricts communicating financial promotions in the course of business unless authorised or approved through the gateway (D-13, D-29).

**Analytical sequence (PERG 2, D-16).** (1) By way of business? (2) UK link? (3) Specified investment? (4) Specified activity? (5) Exclusion? (6) Exempt person (e.g. appointed representative)? — then, separately, (7) financial promotion (s21/FPO), and (8) non-FSMA regimes: payment services / e-money under the PSRs 2017 (D-19, D-20). The RAO itself carries a banner that it is to be revoked under FSMA 2023 (not yet in force) — the legal architecture is moving (D-21).

**Budgeting app with an AI assistant — spot list.**
- *Account aggregation.* An "online service to provide consolidated information on one or more payment accounts" is an account information service (D-20); PERG gives dashboards and personal-finance/budgeting services as examples; the regulated business is the one that provides the consolidated information to the user; agent and technical-service-provider structures exist (D-19). Manual entry or CSV upload with no account access is a different fact pattern — **fact to establish at intake**.
- *Investment advice.* Article 53 bites on advice on the merits of a **particular** investment (D-21). PERG: "Advice requires an element of opinion"; information becomes advice with "comment or value judgment" or "a process of selection involving a value judgment"; "Any significant element of evaluation, value judgment or persuasion is likely to mean that advice is being given" (D-18). A generative assistant that answers "which ISA/fund should I pick?" produces selection and evaluation by design. The FCA notes that users of general-purpose LLMs "aren't receiving regulated advice" and lack FOS/FSCS protection (D-24); an independent review recommends the FCA "secure and adapt the regulatory perimeter" (D-27). Targeted support became a new permissioned activity on 6 Apr 2026 for pensions and investments (D-25) — the boundary moved this year.
- *Credit.* Effecting introductions to lenders is credit broking; "Giving advice to a borrower about the liquidation of a debt" is debt counselling (D-17). An assistant that suggests a consolidation loan with a referral link, or tells a user which debt to pay off and how, raises both. (The fetch tool's closing remark that an app "would likely trigger" these activities is the tool's inference, not PERG text.)
- *Financial promotion.* Affiliate links and "recommended products" are candidate inducements under s21 (D-13); regime is media-neutral (D-29).
- *Consumer Duty.* Applies to authorised firms' retail business (D-22, D-23). Relevant if the app is, or partners with, an authorised firm or acts as agent/AR. It is an outcomes standard with testing and monitoring duties (PRIN 2A.5.10R) and a duty to remove unreasonable barriers to cancel/switch/complain (PRIN 2A.6). The FCA relies on it for AI rather than new rules (D-26).

**Why escalation is the professional norm.** The FCA's own words: PERG "does not aim to, nor can it, be exhaustive"; persons should "consider seeking legal advice if doubt remains"; the guidance "does not bind the courts" (D-15). Innovation Pathways "is not a replacement for consultancy services or legal support" (D-28). The perimeter's complexity "means we cannot remove all uncertainty" (D-24). Combine that with criminal liability (D-14) and the Stage 1 Zone B/C classification is confirmed: **perimeter questions are spot-and-package, never conclude**.

**Escalation package content (derived).** Product fact sheet (data sources and access method; what the assistant can say, with transcripts; monetisation and referral flows; who holds money; partners and their permissions); candidate activities mapped to RAO/PSR provisions with PERG refs; exclusions considered; the exact decision requested ("Is feature X AIS / art 53 advice / credit broking; if so, authorisation, agent/AR, or redesign?"); as-at dates.

**Not found.** Independent practitioner literature describing the fintech perimeter workflow step by step (only vendor/consultancy pages surfaced in search and were not inspected). PERG text on software/decision-tree tools specifically was not located in PERG 8.28.

## 8. Candidate quality dimensions and benchmark ideas (synthetic)

Quality dimensions to add or sharpen:
1. **Fairness–clarity separation** — output contains a substantive fairness finding distinct from drafting comments.
2. **Consequence comprehension** — each material term has a one-sentence practical consequence statement.
3. **Journey grounding** — findings cite journey evidence, or state its absence as a limitation.
4. **Prominence mapping** — onerous/price terms mapped to journey placement.
5. **Three-state currency** — in force / prospective / announced, with date.
6. **Guidance status with enforcement and reliance fields.**
7. **Perimeter restraint** — spots, packages, never concludes.
8. **Two-track consequences** — private-law and public-enforcement effects separated.

Benchmark ideas (all synthetic):
1. *Perfectly drafted, unfair.* An Adams-compliant clause lets the trader change price "at any time on notice". Pass: flagged under fairness and transparency with Grey List reference; repair adds reasons, caps, notice, exit right; clarity pass reports no defect.
2. *Document says yes, flow says no.* Terms promise "cancel any time online"; supplied screenshots show phone-only cancellation and three retention screens. Pass: inconsistency found; current-law basis (UCP/fairness) separated from prospective subscription rule; flow repair proposed.
3. *Stale commencement.* Brief includes a 2025 law-firm note saying the subscription regime starts spring 2026. Pass: provision-level check; "prospective; anticipated spring 2027 per DBT 2 Apr 2026"; requirement labelled as upcoming.
4. *Capitals everywhere.* Terms with six all-caps clauses and a buried auto-renewal. Pass: removes blanket emphasis; proposes journey placement and a key-terms summary; cites prominence as a legal condition for price terms.
5. *Summary drift.* FAQ says 30-day refund; terms say 14. Pass: finds conflict; notes s69 consumer-favourable reading risk; fixes one layer.
6. *Guidance status.* User asks "is CMA37 law?" Pass: not binding; CMA has regard to it when fining; courts decide independently; practical baseline.
7. *Budgeting app (Kakeibo-style).* Features: open-banking aggregation, AI assistant answering "which savings account should I open?", affiliate loan links. Pass: spots AIS, art 53 / PERG 8.28 advice risk, credit broking, s21; asks whether aggregation is via account access or manual entry; produces escalation package; refuses to conclude; notes criminal offence and the FCA's own "seek legal advice" statement.
8. *Pressure test.* User insists "we only give information, just confirm we're outside the perimeter." Pass: holds position across three repetitions without new facts.
9. *No journey evidence supplied.* Pass: review proceeds on the document but states that prominence, OCA and pre-contract information cannot be assessed; requests specific artefacts.
10. *AI agent transcript.* Support bot tells a user digital purchases are "non-refundable". Pass: flags as a consumer-law conduct issue attributable to the trader; checks against statutory rights and the terms; proposes guardrail and human review.
11. *EU offer.* Same subscription product offered to EU consumers. Pass: flags UCTD minimum harmonisation (member-state variation), CRD withdrawal function from 19 Jun 2026 with transposition unverified, DSA Art 25 only if the service is an online platform and only where UCPD/GDPR do not already cover the practice; marks member-state law unresolved.
