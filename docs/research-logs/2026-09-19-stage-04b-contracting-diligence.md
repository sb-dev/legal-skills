## 3. Practice description per stream

### 3.1 Commercial contracting (drafting, review, redlining, amendment)

| Aspect | What the evidence shows | IDs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roles | In-house lawyers, contract/commercial managers, procurement and sales run volume contracting; external firms handle bespoke or high-value work. Playbooks exist so "junior lawyers" and "non-legal teams" can handle low-risk, high-volume paper and escalate the rest. In UK government the contract manager, SRO and budget holder are distinct named roles. | B-40, B-15, B-16 |
| Professional duties (E&W solicitors) | Competent and timely service; not misleading others by act or omission; no unfair advantage over third parties; confidentiality; information the client can understand so it can decide. These apply to the solicitor, not to a tool, but they define what a reviewing human must be able to stand behind. | B-14 |
| Matter intake / review brief | Before mark-up the reviewer needs: which side, whose paper, deal value and risk class, governing law, applicable playbook or policy, authority limits, and the business objective. Vendor and government material both put risk classification and approval thresholds **before** clause work. A formal "review brief" template from a professional body was **not found**. | B-40, B-16, B-39 |
| Fact gathering | Scope, specification, price mechanics and delivery facts come from the business. WorldCC-derived material says scope and goals are where value is most often lost; the lawyer's markup cannot fix an unclear scope. | B-20, B-21 (not independent) |
| Issue spotting | Playbook-driven: match each clause to preferred / fallback / deal-breaker. High-risk set named consistently: limitation of liability, indemnities, IP, termination, confidentiality, payment, data. English-law overlay: UCTA reasonableness for standard-terms limitations; Misrepresentation Act s.3 for non-reliance; third-party rights; NOM/variation formalities; endeavours wording; discretion clauses. | B-39, B-40, B-02, B-01, B-03, B-12, B-04, B-06 |
| Jurisdiction analysis | Governing law changes what words do. Labels that Adams treats as inert (warrants/represents; best/reasonable endeavours; consequential loss) carry doctrinal weight in E&W. | B-04, B-08, B-09, B-10, B-26 |
| Source hierarchy | Statute → appellate authority → first-instance authority → practitioner notes → vendor material. Practitioner notes go stale (see B-30 vs B-12) and must be dated and checked against the primary source. | B-12, B-30 |
| Drafting / review method | Tracked changes only; small separable edits; a comment giving the reason for each substantive change; clean + redline pair; single authoritative draft; retained audit trail. Support for these is **vendor-level only**; no regulator or professional-body standard on redline mechanics was found. | B-39 |
| Standard forms and clause libraries | Three layers observed: (a) organisation playbook and approved fallback wording; (b) cross-industry principles meant to be adopted whole or in part to cut negotiation (WorldCC); (c) locked community standard agreements that are not to be edited (oneNDA). Government adds mandatory policy playbooks and model contracts. | B-40, B-17, B-41, B-16 |
| Filing / registration boundaries | None for ordinary commercial contracts. Execution formalities (deeds) and variation formalities are the nearest analogue. ## 4. Challenge findings

### AD1-02 — rejecting reliance on "tested" contract language

| Field | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Supporting | English courts stress context and that "the same word or phrase may mean different things in different documents" (B-10 [15], TV). *Star Polaris* gave "consequential loss" a non-traditional meaning in context (B-11). Rhodia's language on endeavours is hedged ("probably", "may well be") (B-04). Lewison LJ's dissent in Jet2 shows litigated wording stays uncertain (B-05). So "tested" rarely means "settled". |
| Contrary | For **market standard forms**, the Court of Appeal stresses consistent interpretation for "clarity, certainty and predictability" (B-31, judgment not inspected). Ashurst: terms "have come to acquire an accepted legal sense through decided cases" (B-23). For warrants/represents, the conventional English label and structure **were** decisive (B-08, B-09). Professionally drafted contracts are read mainly textually (B-13), so conventional wording is read as deliberately chosen. |
| Jurisdiction limits | Adams argues from US practice. In E&W, precedent binds on construction of the same standard form far more than on bespoke wording. |
| Changes since the book | None that weaken the point; post-2015 textual emphasis (*Arnold*, *Wood*) raises the cost of departing from a conventional label without express substitute content. |
| Disposition | **Qualify.** Split into: (a) bespoke wording — "tested" is a weak reason; (b) industry standard forms and doctrinal labels — treat as operative and do not rewrite without governing-law research. Keep the Stage 3 gate "does the governing law care about these words?". |
| Standing | Disputed claim; the two-way split is a context-dependent method. |

### AD2-12 — no hierarchy among best / reasonable / all reasonable endeavours

| Field | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Supporting | Rhodia itself suggests "all reasonable" may equal "best" (B-04 [33]) — so the three-tier ladder is not clean; Trinity calls the middle tier's level "uncertain" (B-24). Jet2: content depends "very much on the nature and terms of the contract" (B-05). English practitioners give Adams's repair: specify steps, cost and duration (B-24). |
| Contrary | Rhodia [33]–[34] (TV): "there is a distinction between best endeavours and reasonable endeavours". Reasonable = one reasonable course; best = all reasonable courses. Rhodia [44] as reported: sacrificing commercial interests was no answer to a mandatory obligation. Practitioner notes uniformly teach a spectrum (B-24; several others in search results, not inspected). Adams's own reply post could not be fetched (403). |
| Jurisdiction limits | E&W. First-instance and CA authority; no Supreme Court ruling found. US position not researched here. |
| Changes since the book | *Astor Management v Atalaya Mining* (2017) named by B-24 — not inspected; research question. |
| Disposition | **Reject as a statement of English law; retain the drafting remedy.** Under English governing law an agent must never normalise or equate endeavours formulas. It inventories them, flags mixed use, states that English courts distinguish at least best vs reasonable, and recommends express steps. |
| Standing | English distinction = supported finding (as of 2026-09-19, E&W, authority below Supreme Court). Exact content of each tier = unresolved question. "Be specific instead" = practical heuristic supported on both sides. |

### AD1-24 — replace "represents and warrants" with "states"

| Field | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Supporting | Both Sycamore and Idemitsu turned partly on the **contract as a whole** (separate treatment of representations, disclosure mechanics, entire-agreement wording), not the verb alone; Sycamore [203] notes parties may expressly agree that contractual statements are representations. | Contrary | Misrepresentation Act 1967 ss.1–2 give rescission and tort-measure damages for representations (B-01). ### AD3-05 — consequential loss exclusions

| Field | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Supporting | English law gives "consequential loss" a technical second-limb meaning under a line of cases (B-27), so lost profits are often **direct** and survive the exclusion — the trap Adams describes. ### AD1-20 — "sole discretion" and implied limits

| Field | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Supporting | The method (ask what the client fears; state criteria; obtain express acknowledgments) matches English practitioner advice to draft carefully if a right is meant to be absolute (B-25). | Contrary (to the recorded premise) | The Stage 3 note records Adams as saying England does **not** imply a good-faith duty. As a general duty that is broadly how practitioners describe it, **but** Braganza implies that a contractual discretion be exercised rationally, "as well as in good faith", and consistently with contractual purpose (B-06 [30], TV). | Jurisdiction limits | UK. Braganza and Tesco are employment cases; commercial application is via other cases not inspected (*Mid Essex*, *Socimer*, *Shurbanova*, *TAQA v Rockrose*). |

| Disposition | **Qualify and correct the premise.** For E&W: flag every discretion, determination, approval or opinion clause; ask whether it is a discretion or a claimed absolute right; recommend stated criteria and a decision record; never advise that "sole/absolute" makes the decision unchallengeable. | Standing | Braganza limit = supported finding (UKSC). Scope of the "absolute right" exception = unresolved question. |
