**Project:** `legal-skills`

**Status:** Complete  
**Date:** 12 September 2026  
**Stage 3 input:** `2026-09-12-stage-03-five-book-extraction.md`

## 1. Stage purpose

Stage 3 produced a provisional legal-production model from the five-book corpus. This stage tests that model against current professional practice, authoritative guidance, current official legal sources, failure cases and empirical evidence.

The stage answers two questions:

1. Which book-derived legal-production methods remain defensible, under what conditions and with what limits?
2. Which important professional responsibilities, risks or practices were missing or under-specified in the corpus model?

The aim is not to prove that one universal legal workflow exists. It is to produce an **evidence-qualified legal-production model** that can support later jurisdiction, artefact, workflow, skill and evaluation design without silently promoting book-derived heuristics into professional rules.

The Stage 3 capability model is therefore treated as a hypothesis:

```text
five-book corpus
→ provisional capabilities
→ current professional-practice challenge
→ supporting / contrary / limiting evidence
→ revised capabilities
→ explicit unresolved boundaries
```

Current authoritative law and regulator guidance control current legal propositions. Professional bodies, legal-operations frameworks and empirical research can support practice design but do not become legal authority merely because they are useful.

## 2. Research method and evidence hierarchy

The research was deliberately broader than the books while remaining bounded to the Stage 1 project scope.

For material findings, evidence was classified approximately as:

```text
current legislation / court or tribunal decision
→ regulator / official authoritative guidance
→ current professional regulatory or representative-body practice guidance
→ established professional operations frameworks
→ empirical research
→ practitioner examples / case studies
→ heuristic or hypothesis
```

This ordering is not a universal ranking. The appropriate evidence depends on the claim. For example:

- a proposition about reserved legal activities requires legislation / regulator material;
- a proposition about effective supervision can use current SRA guidance;
- a proposition about legal-design comprehension requires empirical evidence rather than professional enthusiasm;
- a proposition about matter-management implementation may use practitioner case studies but should remain a heuristic.

The research sought contrary evidence and failure cases rather than only confirmation.

# 3. Challenge result: legal production begins before issue spotting

Stage 3 began C1 with matter framing and fact control. Current in-house practice shows that this is still one step too late if the system has not first established **who the legal function is acting for, what it has been asked to do, and whether it can properly act**.

The SRA's in-house guidance and July 2026 case studies repeatedly require attention to:

```text
client / represented entity
scope of instructions
material information available
conflicts or significant conflict risk
confidentiality duties
competence
realistic delivery timescale
authority to approve / decide
```

The case study on internal pressure to approve a contract is particularly revealing. The legal problem is not simply whether an exclusion clause is enforceable. The lawyer must establish whether the supposed policy was ever approved, inspect earlier records, identify the person with actual authority, resist sales pressure and record the rationale for any decision.

## Stage 3 challenge

**Stage 3 claim:** matter framing begins with project request, known facts and objective.

**Finding:** supported but incomplete.

## Revised production rule

Before substantive analysis:

```text
request
→ identify client / matter owner / intended consumer of advice
→ establish instruction and decision scope
→ conflict / confidentiality / competence screen
→ identify required authority / approval
→ establish facts, assumptions and missing evidence
→ legal issue framing
```

Not every non-lawyer user is a formal legal client, and an open-source skill must not manufacture a solicitor-client relationship. The capability should therefore represent **matter owner / advice recipient / decision owner** generically while allowing regulated deployments to use the legally correct client model.

**Disposition:** `ADAPT`  
**Evidential standing:** supported finding.

# 4. Challenge result: supervision must be risk-based and attributable

Stage 3 treated human review as necessary where competence, authority, reserved activity or risk requires another actor. Current SRA guidance adds important precision.

The June 2026 supervision guidance rejects a single supervision model. Appropriate supervision depends on:

- potential harm if work is defective;
- regulatory restrictions on who may perform work;
- judgement intensity and novelty;
- client vulnerability;
- worker competence and workload;
- supervisor capacity;
- available support, guidance and standardised processes.

The supervisor needs meaningful oversight, availability and enough knowledge of matters or samples of work to provide assurance. Accountability remains with the authorised/supervising professional where the regulatory model places it there.

The August 2026 AI warning notice similarly states that use of AI does not transfer professional responsibility. Human oversight must be informed and proportionate to risk.

## Stage 3 challenge

**Stage 3 claim:** human review is not a fallback; escalate where risk or authority requires it.

**Finding:** strongly supported, but “human review” is too vague to be operational.

## Revised production rule

Review should identify:

```text
review owner
why review is required
review depth
material requiring review
source / factual uncertainties
approval or decision being requested
whether work may proceed before approval
```

Risk-based review should distinguish, for example:

```text
routine orientation / low-consequence administrative work
→ bounded self-check + source verification

material advice / novel interpretation / high-impact contract
→ qualified peer or supervising review

reserved activity / filing / representation / high-consequence specialist matter
→ authorised specialist owner and explicit approval
```

This is a **policy shape**, not a universal severity table. The legal and regulatory basis for review varies by deployment.

**Disposition:** `RETAIN + SPECIFY`  
**Evidential standing:** supported finding.

# 5. Challenge result: AI research requires source reconstruction, not citation plausibility

The Stage 3 authority model is strongly confirmed by current professional and judicial evidence.

The SRA's August 2026 warning notice highlights two recurrent risks:

```text
fabricated / inaccurate authorities
confidentiality failures
```

`Ayinde` provides direct failure evidence. The court states that freely available generative AI cannot be treated as a reliable legal-research source and that AI-assisted research must be checked against authoritative sources before professional use. The judgment identifies official legislation, The National Archives' judgment database, official law reports and reputable legal-publisher databases as examples of authoritative verification routes.

This is stricter than attaching a plausible-looking citation to model output.

## Required evidence chain

For a material legal proposition:

```text
model / search candidate
→ resolve real authority identity
→ retrieve adequate source text
→ locate the supporting passage
→ classify source and legal status
→ check current / later treatment where material
→ apply to matter facts
```

A citation parser proving that `[2026] UKSC 10` has valid syntax is not evidence that the case exists or supports the proposition.

## Direct support from current court procedure

Practice Direction 52A requires an authority to be tied to the proposition it demonstrates and to the specific parts supporting that proposition. This reinforces a key Stage 3 decision:

```text
citation alone
≠ proposition evidence
```

**Disposition:** `RETAIN STRONGLY`  
**Evidential standing:** supported by current regulator guidance, court decision and procedure.

# 6. Challenge result: authoritative-source coverage has false-negative failure modes

Stage 3 focused on source quality but under-specified source **coverage**.

The National Archives' Find Case Law provides official judgments and neutral-citation search, but its own guidance warns that a correctly cited judgment may not be present in the collection. Therefore:

```text
not found in one official database
≠ case does not exist
```

Likewise `legislation.gov.uk` warns on live revised legislation pages that revised text may not be fully up to date and surfaces outstanding effects not yet applied editorially.

## New production distinction

Legal research needs to record both:

```text
source authority
and
source coverage / completeness for the question
```

A negative search result is a claim requiring its own evidential basis.

### Example

```text
Find Case Law search: no result
→ check citation formatting / court / date
→ inspect collection coverage
→ use another authoritative/reputable route where necessary
→ only then qualify the negative conclusion
```

**Disposition:** `ADD`  
**Evidential standing:** supported finding.

# 7. Challenge result: post-Brexit UK/EU research cannot inherit the old corpus workflow

The 2011 *Legal Skills* text and the 2019 practitioner handbook contain useful durable research methods but are temporally unsafe as a current map of UK/EU legal status.

The government's final July 2026 retained-EU-law dashboard confirms that:

- retained EU law not revoked by the end of 2023 became **assimilated law**;
- EU principles of interpretation were removed from the domestic assimilated-law regime from 1 January 2024;
- most powers under the Retained EU Law (Revocation and Reform) Act expired on 23 June 2026;
- the dashboard received its final update in July 2026 and is scheduled for decommissioning in January 2027, leaving a downloadable final dataset.

## Stage 3 challenge

**Stage 3 claim:** provider-specific and substantive EU-law details from older books require re-verification.

**Finding:** strongly confirmed.

## Production consequence

Do not encode one permanent “post-Brexit search path”. The workflow needs temporal and source-version awareness:

```text
matter date
+ relevant legal instrument
+ current domestic status
+ later amendments / replacements
+ relevant interpretive regime
→ current proposition
```

Stage 5 must model this without trying to create a universal conflict-of-laws or EU-law engine.

**Disposition:** `RETAIN + STRENGTHEN`  
**Evidential standing:** supported current official evidence.

# 8. Challenge result: risk scoring is useful only when its uncertainty remains visible

The Oxford Handbook supported systematic, cyclical legal-risk management. Stage 3 correctly refused to collapse quality into one score. Current risk practice reinforces that caution.

The 2026 Orange Book permits qualitative or quantitative likelihood assessment and differentiated consequence scales for prioritisation. It also says analysis should consider:

- sensitivity and confidence in available information;
- complexity and connectivity;
- time and volatility;
- effectiveness of existing controls.

Therefore a risk matrix can be useful for prioritisation, but a number cannot replace the underlying analysis.

## Invalid pattern

```text
legal risk = 72 / 100
```

with no inspectable basis for:

```text
uncertain law
uncertain facts
harm / consequence
likelihood assumptions
controls
reversibility
confidence
who owns the decision
```

## Revised production rule

Use risk scores or labels only as **derived decision aids** when the organisation has a defined method. Preserve the components and uncertainty that produced them.

For a project without an established risk taxonomy, Legal Skills should prefer structured dimensions over invented numeric precision.

**Disposition:** `QUALIFY`  
**Evidential standing:** supported context-dependent method.

# 9. Challenge result: product-law traceability has concrete change triggers

Stage 3 proposed legal-product consistency and change-impact capability. Current privacy practice provides unusually strong evidence for how this can work operationally.

The ICO's February 2026 data-protection-by-design guidance requires privacy to be considered from design and throughout the processing lifecycle.

Its DPIA audit framework expects organisations to:

- integrate DPIA outcomes into project plans and risk registers;
- not start high-risk processing before required mitigations are in place;
- communicate outcomes to relevant stakeholders;
- review DPIAs regularly or when the nature, scope, context or purpose of processing changes.

The right-to-be-informed guidance similarly requires privacy information to remain accurate and to be updated before a new use of personal data begins.

## Stage 3 challenge

**Stage 3 claim:** legal artefacts must remain aligned with product behaviour and changed facts/law should trigger dependent review.

**Finding:** strongly supported.

## Concrete traceability pattern

```text
legal proposition / obligation
→ project requirement
→ control / implementation
→ user-facing statement or legal artefact
→ evidence / approval
→ change trigger
```

Candidate change triggers include:

```text
new processing purpose
new category of data
new recipient / processor
new territory / transfer
new automated decision
new commercial promise
new subscription / refund behaviour
new licence dependency
new product market
legal / regulator guidance change
material authority change
control implementation change
```

Not every change requires full re-analysis. The system should identify the affected dependency chain and repair the smallest responsible scope.

**Disposition:** `RETAIN STRONGLY`  
**Evidential standing:** supported finding.

# 10. Challenge result: consumer terms prove that clarity is not legality

Adams supplies strong semantic drafting methods, but his business-contract focus cannot be generalized to consumer contracts.

The CMA's July 2026 unfair-contract guidance requires consumer terms and notices to be both **fair and transparent**. Fairness is contextual: the analysis considers the rights and obligations created, what is being sold, surrounding terms and circumstances.

This produces a useful layered review:

```text
semantic clarity
+
substantive fairness / legality
+
commercial-practice consistency
+
product implementation
```

A beautifully drafted limitation or cancellation term may still fail because the underlying allocation of rights is unlawful or unfair.

The current CMA agentic-AI guidance adds a further product rule: businesses remain responsible when customer-facing AI agents act unlawfully. Delegating a customer interaction to an agent does not delegate legal responsibility.

## Stage 3 challenge

**Stage 3 claim:** clear English is not a substitute for legality.

**Finding:** strongly confirmed.

**Disposition:** `RETAIN STRONGLY`  
**Evidential standing:** supported current regulator guidance.

# 11. Challenge result: approval provenance is a first-class legal artefact

The SRA's July 2026 “internal pressure to approve a contract” case study exposes a failure mode that the books did not model explicitly:

```text
old draft marked “for discussion”
→ organisational memory turns it into supposed policy
→ sales pressure treats it as prior legal approval
→ lawyer is asked to sign off without adequate evidence
```

The correct response includes checking past files and correspondence, identifying a person with appropriate authority, refusing to approve without adequate time/information where necessary, escalating and keeping a written record of concerns and decisions.

## New production concept

A significant legal approval should preserve provenance such as:

```text
what was approved
revision / scope
by whom
in what role / authority
when
against which known facts / assumptions
subject to what conditions
what remains unresolved
what change invalidates the approval
```

This is not a universal corporate-signoff schema. It is a reusable need that later artefact design should represent cheaply.

**Disposition:** `ADD`  
**Evidential standing:** supported professional-regulatory case-study evidence.

# 12. Challenge result: legal design is useful but technique effectiveness must be tested

The Oxford Handbook's proactive legal-design material suggested that user-centred and visual approaches can make legal information more usable. Broader evidence requires a narrower conclusion.

A 2023 empirical study of visual contracts found meaningful transaction-cost benefits in the employment and banking contexts it studied. That supports legal design as a serious production method rather than mere decoration.

However, the UK Behavioural Insights Team's large experimental programme on online terms and privacy policies found that techniques were context-dependent. In one experiment with 1,867 participants, adding illustrative icons, vignettes or text boxes to a full privacy policy did not improve comprehension; the icon treatment reduced comprehension relative to control.

## Stage 3 challenge

**Book-derived tendency:** better visual / legal design improves comprehension and operational adoption.

**Finding:** useful hypothesis, not universal rule.

## Revised production rule

```text
identify user / task / comprehension need
→ choose a representation hypothesis
→ preserve legal meaning
→ test with representative users where consequence warrants
→ retain only evidence-supported improvement
```

Legal design must not become:

```text
add icons
→ declare document understandable
```

Evaluation should measure the intended outcome, such as comprehension, task success, error rate, time-to-answer or operational compliance.

**Disposition:** `QUALIFY`  
**Evidential standing:** context-dependent method with supporting and contrary empirical evidence.

# 13. Challenge result: the professional boundary is activity-based and deployment-dependent

The Legal Services Board confirms six reserved legal activities under the Legal Services Act 2007:

```text
rights of audience
conduct of litigation
reserved instrument activities
probate activities
notarial activities
administration of oaths
```

Section 14 makes unauthorised performance of a reserved activity an offence unless an entitlement or exemption applies.

At the same time, the SRA's updated June 2026 supervision guidance records a Court of Appeal clarification that tasks falling within conduct of litigation may in some circumstances be performed by an unauthorised person **for and on behalf of an authorised individual**, with that authorised individual retaining the conduct of litigation and responsibility under appropriate direction, management, supervision and control.

## Stage 1 / Stage 3 challenge

A simplistic rule such as:

```text
“an unregulated person or AI may never touch a task connected with a reserved activity”
```

would be overbroad.

The correct repository boundary is:

```text
legal-skills itself
≠ authorised legal professional
≠ regulated legal-services entity
≠ independent holder of filing / representation authority
```

It may support tasks inside a properly authorised and supervised workflow where applicable law and professional rules permit. It must not claim the authorisation, decide that a particular delegation is lawful without adequate analysis, or bypass the authorised actor responsible for the reserved work.

**Disposition:** `QUALIFY BOUNDARY`  
**Evidential standing:** supported current regulator guidance; matter-specific scope can require specialist advice.

# 14. Challenge result: consumer-facing legal tools are a distinct product class

The Stage 1 default was internal/project legal-production support rather than autonomous public legal advice. Current LSB research strongly supports keeping this distinction.

The LSB's June 2026 work found that standards specifically designed for direct-to-consumer AI legal tools are sparse, and highlighted protection gaps around vulnerable users, specialist/regulatory routing and redress. Its consumer research identified strong expectations around accuracy, informed consent before consequential action, human oversight, redress and personal-data protection.

The CMA's updated guidance for unregulated legal-services providers separately shows that a provider's description of its service, regulatory status, competence, limits and protections can itself create consumer-law risk.

## Production consequence

A project exposing Legal Skills directly to consumers needs a deployment-specific analysis covering at least:

```text
service claims
regulated / unregulated status
reserved activity boundary
competence / scope
vulnerability
consequential action / consent
human support / escalation
complaints / redress
privacy and data handling
jurisdiction
consumer terms / sales practices
```

These concerns must not be hidden behind a generic “not legal advice” footer.

**Disposition:** `RETAIN BOUNDARY + ADD DEPLOYMENT GATE`  
**Evidential standing:** supported current regulator/research evidence.

# 15. Challenge result: confidentiality and privilege need explicit information routing

Stage 3 included professional boundary but did not give confidentiality and privilege their own capability surface.

The SRA's August 2026 AI warning notice requires practitioners to understand the contractual and technical safeguards of AI systems and to protect client confidentiality. It warns that both free and paid systems may retain or use submitted information depending on provider terms, settings and architecture.

Current in-house guidance also stresses that not every communication involving an in-house solicitor is automatically legally privileged; status depends on the nature, purpose and recipients of the communication.

## Production consequence

Before material matter content is sent to a tool:

```text
classify information sensitivity
→ determine confidentiality / privilege concern
→ determine approved execution environment
→ minimize / redact where appropriate
→ verify provider retention / training / access / location controls where material
→ preserve matter-specific restriction
```

The repository must not encode provider assumptions such as:

```text
“paid AI = confidential”
```

or:

```text
“anything sent to an AI automatically waives privilege”
```

Those are deployment- and fact-dependent propositions.

**Disposition:** `ADD FIRST-CLASS CAPABILITY`  
**Evidential standing:** supported regulatory guidance; privilege analysis remains jurisdiction/fact specific.

# 16. Challenge result: legal operations belongs in core only where it changes legal-production quality

CLOC's Core 12 and current Law Society material show that modern legal operations includes matter intake, knowledge management, information governance, service delivery, technology, training, project management, metrics and vendor management.

That does **not** justify turning `legal-skills` into a legal-department ERP or matter-management product.

The Which? matter-management case study is useful because it illustrates a narrower principle:

```text
map and improve the legal workflow
before
selecting / automating the technology
```

The resulting system captured legal requests, matter status, renewal notifications and management data, and it was user-tested by both lawyers and business users.

## Core boundary

Retain reusable legal-production operations such as:

```text
structured intake
matter status / responsibility
approval / decision record
source / evidence provenance
review / renewal trigger
knowledge reuse with provenance
service handoff
```

Do not own:

```text
legal budget management
outside-counsel billing
enterprise vendor management
staffing / HR
full legal-department analytics
universal matter database
```

Those are legal-operations concerns but not automatically Legal Skills core responsibilities.

**Disposition:** `ADAPT / BOUND`  
**Evidential standing:** professional practice framework + practitioner example.

# 17. Challenge result: specialised legal streams share a method, not one substantive-law core

Stage 4 sampled multiple professional streams required by the bootstrap specification. They share evidence and production disciplines but not one body of substantive law.

## 17.1 In-house product / technology counsel

Recurring pattern:

```text
understand project / business context
→ enter early enough to influence design
→ identify legal issues / constraints / options
→ maintain independence
→ trace material requirements into implementation
→ preserve approval and escalation routes
```

Strong evidence: SRA in-house guidance, SRA contract-pressure case study, Law Society in-house protocol, ICO privacy-by-design guidance.

**Core status:** strong proving stream.

## 17.2 Commercial contracting

Recurring pattern:

```text
commercial objective / deal facts
→ legal / operational risk allocation
→ clause function
→ enforceability / legality / policy constraints
→ redline / negotiation
→ approval
→ obligations / renewal / change tracking
```

Adams remains useful for language function and ambiguity but does not determine UK substantive contract effect or consumer fairness.

**Core status:** strong proving stream; complex transactions remain specialist.

## 17.3 Privacy / data protection

Current practice strongly supports:

- design-stage legal involvement;
- data-flow and actor mapping;
- explicit risk assessment;
- documented mitigations and approval;
- lifecycle review and change triggers;
- privacy notice ↔ actual processing consistency.

**Core status:** strong proving stream and likely Extension Pack / specialisation candidate because substantive detail is large and change-sensitive.

## 17.4 Consumer / digital commerce

Current CMA guidance demonstrates that:

- wording, substantive fairness and commercial practice all matter;
- presentation and prominence can be legally material;
- product behaviour such as cancellation, pricing or agentic customer interactions must match legal obligations.

**Core status:** proving stream; substantive consumer-law profile should remain evidence-driven and jurisdiction-specific.

## 17.5 Intellectual property and trade marks

The reusable method includes:

```text
asset / proposed use
→ ownership / provenance evidence
→ territory / right / class / scope
→ permission / licence / exception
→ conflict / clearance evidence
→ specialist filing / enforcement boundary
```

Trade mark registration and material clearance decisions require registry evidence and may justify professional advice; Legal Skills must not present a simple name search as full clearance.

**Core status:** method in core; specialist substantive packs likely.

## 17.6 Copyright / licensing / media

Current UK government material reinforces that use commonly turns on ownership, permission/licence or applicable exception. The March 2026 copyright-and-AI report demonstrates that AI/copyright policy remains change-sensitive and should not be frozen into durable prompts.

**Core status:** provenance/licence method in core; substantive copyright/media specialisations beyond core baseline.

## 17.7 Open-source compliance

Government engineering standards provide a practical evidence chain:

```text
dependency / code asset
→ licence identity and version
→ ownership / copyright
→ permissions / conditions / obligations
→ distribution / modification context
→ notices / source / attribution obligations
→ evidence of compliance
```

The licence text and applicable law matter more than a package manager's licence label alone.

**Core status:** strong cross-domain use case; likely specialised pack because licence families and distribution contexts require depth.

## 17.8 Financial-services / fintech regulatory analysis

The FCA Handbook demonstrates why regulatory research needs version and change awareness. It exposes a complete consolidated set of FCA legal instruments and supports comparing text over time and update alerts. Dual-regulated firms may also need PRA rules.

**Core status:** generic regulatory research/change-control method in core; financial-services doctrine is specialist and must not be claimed by default.

## 17.9 AI / automated-system regulation

The 2026 environment itself demonstrates temporal instability:

- ICO automated-decision guidance is being revised following the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025;
- copyright/AI policy was reported to Parliament in March 2026;
- CMA published agentic-AI consumer guidance in March 2026;
- SRA published its AI warning notice in August 2026.

**Core status:** change-aware legal method in core; no frozen universal “AI law” checklist.

## 17.10 Legal research and memorandum practice

Current court procedure and the `Ayinde` failure case strongly support:

```text
question
→ authority
→ specific supporting passage
→ current status
→ application
→ counterargument / uncertainty
→ concise evidence package
```

**Core status:** central.

## 17.11 Legal operations / matter management

Relevant core contribution is operational traceability and routing, not enterprise legal-department management.

**Core status:** bounded supporting capability.

## 17.12 Transactional due diligence

The corpus did not deeply research M&A / corporate transaction practice, and Stage 1 did not place complex corporate transactions in the initial core.

The reusable cross-domain method is still visible:

```text
scope / materiality threshold
→ evidence request
→ provenance / completeness
→ issue / exception finding
→ risk / dependency
→ decision / specialist escalation
```

But deal-specific diligence taxonomies, disclosure mechanics, warranties, tax, competition and transaction structuring are **not** validated as core competence here.

**Core status:** generic evidence-review method only; substantive transaction work remains specialist.

## 17.13 Regulatory compliance / controls

Privacy, consumer and financial-services sources all support a loop such as:

```text
rule / obligation
→ control requirement
→ control owner
→ implementation
→ evidence
→ monitoring / change trigger
```

This is compatible with Legal Skills traceability but must not turn the repository into a generic GRC platform.

**Core status:** legal-to-control traceability in core; operational control execution belongs to consuming projects / relevant domains.

# 20. Missing-capability analysis

Stage 4 identifies several responsibilities that were absent or under-specified in the book-derived model.

## M1 — Client / matter-owner and instruction control

Need to identify:

```text
who requested the work
who the legal client is where applicable
who owns the project decision
what question is actually authorised
who may receive the output
```

This is needed before substantive analysis.

## M2 — Conflict and independence screening

The system needs a reusable ability to surface possible conflict / independence issues and route them to the appropriate professional decision-maker.

It should not pretend to resolve all professional-conflict doctrine generically.

## M3 — Confidentiality / privilege / information-governance routing

Sensitive material must be routed according to matter sensitivity and execution environment. Privilege must not be assumed from the presence of a lawyer or legal tool.

## M4 — Approval provenance

Legal advice, policy positions and contract deviations require traceable decision status so that a provisional discussion draft is not later mistaken for approved policy.

## M5 — Source-coverage awareness

Research must distinguish:

```text
no relevant source exists
from
not found in this source / collection / search
```

## M6 — Deployment / service-claim boundary

Internal support, regulated professional support and consumer-facing legal service are materially different deployment contexts.

## M7 — Review and supervision policy

Human involvement needs an explicit risk-based reason, owner and scope rather than a generic “human in the loop” checkbox.

## M8 — Change-trigger inventory

Legal analysis should identify the factual, product, authority or control changes that invalidate or require review of the result.

## M9 — Matter decision record

For material decisions, preserve:

```text
question
facts / assumptions
authorities
analysis
options / risks
decision / approval owner
outcome
conditions / expiry / review trigger
```

The representation can remain lightweight Markdown unless later requirements justify more.

## M10 — Specialist-domain boundary registry

The project needs a way to distinguish:

```text
core method demonstrated
from
substantive specialist competence researched
from
specialisation planned / unresearched
```

This should later align with Extension Pack status rather than allowing the core to imply competence everywhere.

# 21. Evidence-qualified legal-production capability model

This model supersedes the **provisional** Stage 3 capability model for later bootstrap stages. It remains a capability model, not a proposed skill count.

## Q1 — Matter intake, ownership and factual control

Behaviour:

- identify request, intended outcome and advice recipient;
- identify formal client / represented entity where the deployment requires one;
- identify matter and decision owners;
- establish instructions and scope;
- screen for obvious competence, conflict and independence concerns requiring professional review;
- separate verified facts, assertions, inferences, assumptions, disputes and unknowns;
- identify missing evidence.

Outputs may include:

```text
matter brief
fact / assumption inventory
scope / owner record
open questions
```

## Q2 — Professional, deployment and decision-right boundary

Behaviour:

- identify whether the use is internal support, regulated-professional support or consumer-facing service;
- identify decisions / approvals reserved to another actor;
- identify filing, registration, representation or reserved-activity boundaries;
- distinguish legal analysis from commercial / product decision ownership;
- prevent the system from claiming regulated professional status.

## Q3 — Jurisdiction, temporal and source-scope framing

Behaviour:

- identify potentially relevant legal systems / territories and dates;
- identify current-versus-historical question;
- identify regulator / forum / registry where material;
- identify source coverage limitations and cross-border uncertainty;
- defer detailed data model to Stage 5.

## Q4 — Issue decomposition and research planning

Behaviour:

```text
matter
→ issues / subissues
→ proposition questions
→ evidence needs
→ known-item / subject search strategy
→ source priority
```

Do not begin expensive or broad research before the question is sufficiently bounded.

## Q5 — Authority retrieval, verification, currency and coverage

Behaviour:

- retrieve adequate authoritative material;
- resolve source identity and supporting passage;
- distinguish primary authority, regulator material, official guidance and commentary;
- check amendments, commencement, later treatment or procedural history where material;
- record source / collection limitations;
- qualify negative research results;
- never treat model output as authority.

## Q6 — Proposition extraction and applicability reasoning

Behaviour:

- state legal proposition independently;
- map proposition to supporting passage;
- identify binding / persuasive / informative status where knowable;
- identify material facts and conditions;
- distinguish precedent from analogy;
- identify contrary authority / counterargument where material;
- apply the relevant jurisdiction's interpretive methods;
- expose residual uncertainty rather than converting it to model confidence.

## Q7 — Legal risk, options and uncertainty

Behaviour:

- distinguish legal, factual, temporal, jurisdictional, implementation and evidence uncertainty;
- identify credible consequences and reversibility;
- identify controls / mitigations and their owners;
- present lawful options and decision consequences;
- use organisational risk scales only where defined and preserve underlying dimensions / confidence.

## Q8 — Drafting, review, redlining and approval provenance

Behaviour:

- derive drafting from established matter objective, facts and legal constraints;
- identify intended legal / operational function;
- diagnose ambiguity, insufficient specificity, mistake, conflict, omission and useful/defective vagueness;
- distinguish language quality from substantive legality / fairness;
- preserve acceptable text and repair the smallest responsible unit;
- identify material deviations requiring approval;
- record approval status / provenance and invalidation conditions.

## Q9 — Legal-to-project / control traceability and change impact

Behaviour:

```text
authority / proposition
→ legal requirement / option
→ project requirement
→ implementation / control
→ user-facing representation / legal artefact
→ evidence / owner
→ change trigger
```

On change, recompute only the dependent legal and project scope.

## Q10 — Legal communication and evidence packaging

Behaviour:

- communicate at the fidelity required by the next actor;
- preserve authority and reasoning traceability;
- state assumptions, uncertainty and decision points;
- translate legal requirements into actionable project language without changing meaning;
- use legal-design representations as hypotheses to be tested when user comprehension matters.

## Q11 — Confidentiality, privilege and execution routing

Behaviour:

- classify material sensitivity;
- identify confidentiality / privilege concerns requiring professional analysis;
- minimise or redact inputs where appropriate;
- choose an approved environment based on current provider controls and matter requirements;
- avoid exposing private material merely for convenience;
- preserve restrictions on downstream sharing.

## Q12 — Supervision, escalation and legal-matter lifecycle

Behaviour:

- choose review depth according to risk, novelty, judgement and consequence;
- identify a competent review / approval owner;
- record review outcome and rationale where material;
- produce a precise specialist escalation package;
- preserve matter status, review / renewal triggers and key decision provenance;
- close or supersede stale legal work rather than leaving it implicitly active forever.

# 24. Terminology glossary for later stages

This is a working production glossary, not a complete legal dictionary.

| Term | Stage 4 meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Authority | A source with legal or recognised interpretive status relevant to a proposition; status depends on jurisdiction and context. |
| Authoritative source | A source appropriate to establish or verify a proposition for the matter, e.g. legislation, judgment, regulator material or official registry evidence as applicable. |
| Controlling / binding authority | Authority a decision-maker is required to follow in the relevant context, subject to jurisdiction-specific doctrine. |
| Persuasive authority | Material that may influence but does not control the decision in the same way. |
| Neutral citation | Court-assigned identifier for a judgment / decision, distinct from a publisher's report citation. |
| Source coverage | What a database, registry or collection actually contains; absence from a source is not automatically absence in law. |
| Later treatment | Subsequent procedural or precedential treatment affecting how a judgment may be relied upon. |
| Valid-as-of | Date for which the legal analysis claims current applicability. |
| Matter owner | Person / team responsible for progressing the legal work in the consuming context. |
| Decision owner | Actor authorised to make the relevant product, commercial, legal or professional decision. |
| Approval provenance | Evidence showing what was approved, by whom, in what role, when and subject to what assumptions / conditions. |
| Reserved legal activity | An activity restricted under the Legal Services Act 2007 to entitled or exempt persons. |
| Supervision | Professional oversight whose required form and depth depend on context, work risk and applicable regulation. |
| Conflict | A professional or interest conflict that may prevent or constrain acting; exact doctrine remains deployment-specific. |
| Confidentiality | Duty / restriction controlling disclosure of matter information. |
| Legal professional privilege | A legal protection whose application depends on the communication, purpose, parties and jurisdiction; not synonymous with confidentiality. |
| Legal risk | Effect of legal uncertainty / event / exposure on objectives, considered with consequence, controls and decision context. |
| Risk appetite / tolerance | Organisational decision framework for acceptable risk; not to be invented by the Legal Skills core. |
| Legal design | User-centred design of legal information / artefacts; effectiveness must be evaluated against the intended task. |
| Legal operations | Operational practices enabling legal-service delivery; only the subset affecting reusable legal production belongs in core. |
| Change trigger | Event that may invalidate or require review of a legal conclusion, requirement, control or artefact. |
| Evidence package | Concise, inspectable set of facts, authorities, analysis, uncertainty, decision / approval and dependent artefacts sufficient for the next actor. |

# 25. Failure taxonomy and smallest-responsible repair

## F1 — Wrong client / owner / instruction scope

**Failure:** correct legal analysis is delivered for the wrong entity, question or decision-maker.

**Repair:** re-establish matter scope and re-run only affected issue analysis.

## F2 — Conflict / independence failure

**Failure:** legal work proceeds despite an unmanaged conflict or internal pressure distorting advice.

**Repair:** stop affected work, route to appropriate professional decision-maker, preserve unaffected factual material where lawful.

## F3 — Confidentiality / privilege exposure

**Failure:** sensitive material is sent to an unsuitable tool or shared with an inappropriate recipient.

**Repair:** stop further disclosure, preserve incident evidence, escalate according to applicable professional / security / data process; do not assume privilege can simply be restored.

## F4 — Fabricated or misattributed authority

**Failure:** source does not exist, quotation is false or source does not support proposition.

**Repair:** resolve source against authoritative repository; remove failed proposition; recompute dependent analysis.

## F5 — Stale authority

**Failure:** proposition relies on repealed/amended law, superseded guidance or a case with material later treatment.

**Repair:** update authority status and all dependent propositions / requirements / artefacts.

## F6 — False negative research

**Failure:** “no authority / registration / conflict exists” inferred from one incomplete source or weak search.

**Repair:** inspect source coverage, refine search, use appropriate additional source and qualify remaining uncertainty.

## F7 — Fact laundering

**Failure:** assertion or model inference becomes an unstated “fact”.

**Repair:** restore fact status / provenance and condition dependent legal conclusions.

## F8 — Risk-score laundering

**Failure:** numeric or traffic-light result hides uncertain assumptions or inconsistent scales.

**Repair:** expose dimensions, evidence, controls and confidence; recompute or remove score.

## F9 — Approval laundering

**Failure:** discussion draft, old precedent or prior conditional approval is represented as current authorisation.

**Repair:** resolve approval provenance and obtain current decision where needed.

## F10 — Template-first drafting

**Failure:** precedent drives terms instead of the matter / deal / product and applicable law.

**Repair:** restate intended function and constraints, then redraft affected provisions.

## F11 — Consumer clarity without fairness

**Failure:** easy-to-read term remains substantively unfair / unlawful or inconsistent with sales behaviour.

**Repair:** legal/substantive review, not stylistic editing alone.

## F12 — Legal-design cargo cult

**Failure:** icons, summaries or visual treatment adopted because they look user-friendly without evidence they improve the intended task.

**Repair:** define usability criterion and test; revert or redesign based on evidence.

## F13 — Product / legal drift

**Failure:** implementation changes while policy, notice, terms or prior advice remains unchanged.

**Repair:** traverse dependency map from changed fact/control to affected legal work.

## F14 — Over-delegation / unauthorised execution

**Failure:** agent or unauthorised person makes a filing, representation or professional decision without lawful authority / supervision.

**Repair:** stop execution, identify authorised owner, package work for review and determine whether the task may lawfully be delegated.

## F15 — Generic escalation

**Failure:** “ask a lawyer” transfers discovery cost to the reviewer.

**Repair:** produce precise question, facts, authorities, analysis, uncertainty, options and decision needed.

## F16 — Tool-first legal operations

**Failure:** software is introduced before the legal workflow / ownership / data need is understood.

**Repair:** map current and target process, then choose the smallest supporting tool.

# 26. Candidate evaluation dimensions after Stage 4

Stage 3's multidimensional quality model remains valid and is extended by professional-practice evidence.

Later benchmarks should distinguish at least:

1. **matter / instruction correctness** — correct owner, client/context and question;
2. **fact integrity** — assertions, evidence, assumptions and unknowns remain distinct;
3. **conflict / independence handling** — material professional conflicts or pressure are surfaced appropriately;
4. **source authority** — source type/status fits the proposition;
5. **source fidelity** — proposition is supported by the actual source passage;
6. **source coverage awareness** — negative search conclusions respect collection limits;
7. **temporal validity** — commencement, amendments, later treatment and valid-as-of are correct where material;
8. **jurisdiction fit** — law / regulator / forum / territory is appropriate;
9. **reasoning quality** — rule, precedent, analogy, interpretation and application are handled correctly;
10. **uncertainty calibration** — unresolved law/fact/evidence issues remain inspectable;
11. **risk reasoning** — consequence, controls, confidence and options are transparent rather than hidden by a score;
12. **drafting semantics** — provisions perform intended legal functions and avoid avoidable uncertainty;
13. **substantive legality / fairness** — clear language is not mistaken for legal validity;
14. **approval provenance** — material decisions and deviations have traceable status;
15. **project / control consistency** — legal artefact and real implementation agree;
16. **change impact** — material changes trigger bounded re-review of dependencies;
17. **communication usability** — next actor can understand and act without legal meaning drift;
18. **confidentiality / information routing** — sensitive content is handled in an appropriate environment;
19. **professional boundary** — reserved, specialist and regulated decisions are owned by the correct actor;
20. **supervision / review adequacy** — review depth and reviewer competence are proportionate to the work;
21. **repair discipline** — correction targets the owning failure layer while preserving verified work.

Do not combine these into one legal-quality score merely for dashboard convenience.
