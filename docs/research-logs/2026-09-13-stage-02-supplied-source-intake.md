## 3. What the complementary works add

### S1 — A Manual of Style for Contract Drafting

The supplied fourth edition is established by U06's cover, copyright page (PDF page 4) and preface (PDF pages 34–36). The original comparison considered the fifth edition as A1; the fourth edition now becomes an available, retained supplementary source. It must not be cited as the 2023 edition.

The visible contents and preface identify detailed treatment of contract-language categories, definitions, uncertainty, time, ambiguity and amendments. This strengthens the reading agenda for diagnosing a clause and making a precise correction. It also permits direct comparison with the conceptual and workflow approach proposed for B3, whose supplied material is currently a summary.

**Targeted reading:** Chapters 3, 6–7, 10, 12 and 18, with the annotated revision example where useful. Prefer the book's numbered paragraphs to PDF pagination for findings from this reflowed copy. Evaluate whether a proposed language change preserves the intended allocation of obligations and risk. Do not assume that stylistic advice determines enforceability or that Adams and Stark/Llorente necessarily agree.

### S2 — Legal Research: A Practitioner's Handbook

The [publisher record](https://www.wildy.com/isbn/9780854902651/legal-research-a-practitioner-s-handbook-2nd-ed-hardback-wildy-simmonds-and-hill-publishing-d2123b53-098f-457e-9a61-65cfe80b581c) confirms the **third edition, July 2019**, Emily Allbon and Ian Hunter, with Peter Clinch as editor; ISBN **9780854902651**, Wildy, Simmonds and Hill Publishing. The URL retains an older-edition slug; the displayed title and ISBN identify the third edition.

U05 and the publisher's description identify a practical sequence from problem analysis through source choice and searching to presentation of results. They also identify information purchasing and use of internal precedents. This adds a practitioner perspective alongside B1's teaching text and B2's reasoning focus.

**Targeted reading:** Obtain the sections on planning a research question, choosing and evaluating sources, search strategy, presenting results and internal precedents. Use the material to investigate reproducibility, source suitability and communication to a reviewer. Verify current database behaviour and applicable court requirements separately; a 2019 handbook cannot establish them for 2026. Until direct sections are available, U05 is a research agenda rather than an extracted method.

### S3 — The Oxford Handbook of Law and Management

Oxford's [copyright page](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/63045/chapter/566467753) confirms the four editors, **2026** publication and print ISBN **9780197769034**. Its [book record and contents](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/63045) identify five parts. The supplied two-page overview describes the first two but is not a complete contents map.

This addition creates a stronger line of investigation into how legal work affects organisational decisions and usable product or contract design. The publisher contents identify proactive contracting, legal knowledge and communication, legal design, contract management and systems thinking. Relevant reading targets include chapters 5–6, 15–16, 20–25 and 42; this is a targeted agenda, not a requirement to extract the whole edited volume.

**Targeted comparison:** Test whether these contributions help connect a legal requirement to a decision, document or observed behaviour. Compare their organisational and strategic assumptions with B4 and B5, including who benefits from an option and whether commercial pressure changes the treatment of legal constraints. U04's broad strategic claims are not adopted as operating rules. Neither the summary nor the contents establishes that traceability, change impact or human review has been solved.
