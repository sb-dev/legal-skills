### 3.3 Risk communication and escalation (both streams)

**What is officially done in the UK government legal profession (A-16, 8 Sep 2026; read in full).**

1. "'Legal risk' means the risk that a decision or act is unlawful." (para 5)
2. Three factors are kept **separate** and the advice "will need to be clear on the distinction": (a) likelihood a challenge would succeed — lawyer-led; (b) likelihood a challenge is brought — policy-led; (c) impact — policy-led. (paras 6–13)
3. Even where an issue is unlikely to be tested, "you should always consider what a court would be likely to decide should it address the question." (para 6)
4. A hard floor: "You must advise that a proposed course of action is unlawful if… there is no tenable legal argument that could be put to a court." Before giving that advice, consult line manager and Legal Director. (para 8)
5. Above the floor, the decision is for the Minister. Percentage bands are mandatory aids: <30%, 30–50%, 50–70%, >70%, each with a fixed narrative ("we are significantly more likely to lose if challenged"). (para 19 and summary table)
6. Full merits advice is expected — "not just focus on whether a tenable legal argument is available". (para 18)
7. Mitigations and even law-reform options must be offered. (paras 14–15)
8. Fixed escalation triggers (para 21) and an immediate escalation if the client proposes to proceed without a tenable argument (para 22).
9. "Any subsequent changes [to the legal-risk section of a submission] must be checked by a lawyer". (para 17)

The 2015 predecessor (A-15) explains **why** bands were adopted: lawyers used "percentages… weak/good/strong… a traffic light system", so that "a red or high chance of losing" could mean "55% to one lawyer and 90% to another". That is documented evidence that unanchored verbal or RAG ratings miscommunicate.

**In corporate in-house practice (A-10, 2015, 34 interviews).** Legal-risk management was "often less organised", "ad hoc rather than systematic", relying on senior lawyers' intuition; strategic-level risk reporting is "likely to be reductive" and uses "graphical and quantitative understandings somewhat alien to most lawyers". 81% of surveyed in-house lawyers agreed their job is to help set risk appetite within legal bounds; 77% agreed it is to manage to a known risk appetite (A-09, p. 44).

**Escalation (E&W solicitors; A-02, A-05, A-01).** Report concerns internally — first to the instructing person or manager, then up, possibly "directly to the governing board". Consider an external report where wrongdoing persists. Keep a written record of concerns, actions, outcome and reasons. The SRA is now a prescribed person under PIDA (June 2026 update). The SRA also sets the lower bound: "you do not need to make a report simply because your legal advice is not followed", and the organisation may have "taken an entirely proper decision to accept a degree of legal risk" (W-quotes).

**When to go to outside counsel.** No source gives a rule. Indicators found: work type, volume, risk profile, skill level and cost (A-22); novel cases triaged to senior specialists (A-31); fixed trigger lists in government (A-16 para 21). Evidence that outside advice is sought repeatedly on the same point without capture (A-23) is a legal-ops failure, not an escalation rule.

## 4. Challenge findings

### MVG-OV-03 — the "legal risk" frame turns a legal boundary into a price

| Field | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Supporting evidence | **Authors' own report (A-10, L):** "legality" in practice "often meant something different: not risking serious criminal sanction and having a defensible, if sometimes weak (or risky) argument"; lawyers looked "to the business for a sense of risk appetite… not suggestive of independence"; risk mitigation can be "purely defensive… without necessarily changing the underlying behaviours". **Independent of the authors:** A-15/A-16 show the frame institutionalised: above a minimal "tenable argument" floor, a >70% chance of losing is still "proper and constitutional" to proceed with, and likelihood of challenge is a stated factor. A-09 n. 30 quotes a former Permanent Secretary on the shift from "you can't do that" to "there will be a legal challenge, and it's quite likely we'll lose". A-04: one in four in-house leaders reported pressure to change advice for commercial reasons. |
| Contrary evidence | (1) The risk frame is the **official, current** method for UK government lawyers (A-16), adopted to fix a real defect — inconsistent verbal ratings (A-15). (2) The SRA accepts that an organisation may take "an entirely proper decision to accept a degree of legal risk" and that advice not being followed is not itself reportable (A-02). (3) A-18 (former Treasury Solicitor): it is for the client to decide; the lawyer's failing is false comfort, not the act of rating. (4) Much law is genuinely uncertain; a rating is then the honest form (A-16 para 4). |
| Reading of the conflict | The claim is right about a **failure mode**, wrong if read as a rejection of risk expression. The official model answers it with two devices: a **floor** (no tenable argument → "unlawful", stated as such, escalated) and **factor separation** (merits vs likelihood of challenge vs impact, never blended). |
| Jurisdiction limits | UK. A-16 governs government lawyers only; it is not binding on private-sector counsel and its "tenable argument" floor is set for a public-law context with ministerial accountability. It should **not** be copied as the threshold for consumer-facing product compliance. A-10: large UK corporates, 2015. |
| Changes since the book (2018) | SRA in-house guidance suite (2024; updated 2026); SRA Principles version of 11 Apr 2025; AG guidance rewritten 2022 → Nov 2024 → **8 Sep 2026** (the 2026 text stresses "solutions", agility and that a decision "is only unlawful where no tenable legal argument exists" — A-17). The 2024 text was not inspected, so the exact differences are **not verified**. |
| Disposition | **Retain, qualified** — as a named failure mode plus the two safeguards. Merge with WG-OV-06 under the Stage 3 handling: (1) state what the law requires separately from exposure; (2) a clear requirement is never expressed only as a likelihood; (3) ranges allowed for truly uncertain outcomes; (4) no composite score. **Add (5):** if numbers or RAG are used they must be anchored to defined bands with fixed narrative, and merits, likelihood of enforcement and impact are reported as separate fields. |
| Evidential standing | Supported finding (that the failure mode occurs). Context-dependent method (the remedy). The bootstrap default "no single score" is **consistent** with A-16 (three separate factors, no composite) — but a blanket "no numbers" rule would be **contradicted** by A-15/A-16. |

### SRA position on in-house solicitors (Q9) — summary as at 18–19 Sep 2026, England and Wales

- A dedicated guidance suite exists: identifying your client; reporting concerns about wrongdoing; legal professional privilege; internal investigations; guidance for employers on a solicitor's obligations; key points for governing boards; case studies on conflicts, independence and confidentiality (23 Jul 2026). Drafts March 2024; issued November 2024; reporting-concerns guidance updated 2 June 2026. (A-03, A-01, A-02, A-05, A-06, A-07)
- **Client:** the organisation itself, ultimately represented by its most senior decision-making body; not the instructing individual. Acting for group companies or colleagues is possible only without conflict or significant risk of one. (A-01)
- **Independence:** Principle 3; silence under colleague pressure "may breach your duty to act with independence"; do not approve under time pressure what you have not been able to review. (A-08, A-02, A-06)
- **Reporting lines:** the SRA does not prescribe a reporting line. It asks for "clear channels by which they can raise and escalate any concerns", access up to the governing board where needed, and no punishment for raising concerns. (A-01, A-02, A-05). The Law Society framework (May 2025) reportedly recommends regular board access for the most senior in-house solicitor — search snippet only; **not inspected**.
- **Records:** keep a written record of concerns, actions, outcome and reasons. (A-02)
- This confirms MVG-OV-11's direction (structure over exhortation) from an independent regulator source, and shows the regulator acted after the book.
